# LearnEnglish selected grammar explanations

## Key takeaway
Selected British Council grammar explanations support possibility, request forms, auxiliary combinations and retrospective temporal interpretation within their stated limits.

## Summary
Selected LearnEnglish explanations support preserving uncertainty, recognizing a could-you request and distinguishing auxiliary constructions and perfect-aspect readings. They do not establish universal politeness rankings, a full grammar or learner outcomes.

## Possibility

[Probability, opening Possibility section](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/english-grammar-reference/probability) explains *may*, *might* and *could* for possible rather than certain events. The Practice uses this narrowly for a contextually specified tentative update. It does not assign numerical probabilities or claim that *may* always expresses possibility.

## Requests

[Requests, offers and invitations, Requests section](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/english-grammar-reference/requests-offers-invitations) presents *could you* and *would you* constructions for requests. This supports identifying request use despite interrogative form. The Practice's colleague scenario is an authored application; it does not import the lesson's simplified politeness comparisons as a universal ranking.

## Verb chains

On 2026-10-14, inspected the basic-form table and numbered patterns 1–7 of [Verb phrases](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/english-grammar-reference/verb-phrases). They support HAVE with a past participle, progressive BE with an ing form, passive BE with a past participle, and selected combined chains. The Model's local-selection analysis and printer examples are authored applications.

The lesson's four-part introductory table is not a complete paradigm inventory. Its narrow verb-chain presentation is distinguished from the Model's VP constituent, which includes dependents. The source does not establish every adjectival/passive distinction, dialectal chain or semantic interpretation. The advanced section, exercises and comments are not used as evidence; Link verbs and Relative pronouns pages were not successfully inspected and are not support for this revision.

## Perfect aspect

On 2026-10-14, inspected the introduction and Present perfect and Past perfect explanations in [Perfect aspect](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/english-grammar-reference/perfect-aspect). They relate the perfect to looking back from a reference time and include situations continuing to that time. This supports rejecting “perfect means completed” as a general rule. The Model's E/R/S diagram and residence example are authored illustrations; the page does not establish a full interval semantics or situation-type classification. The later modal-perfect section is outside this contribution.

## Nonclaims

These are pedagogical descriptions, not interactional data from the proposed editing cases. The source does not establish that a particular colleague will judge a request polite or respond favorably. Other sections, exercise results and user comments do not supply evidence for this record. No full grammar inspection or independent review of the package is claimed.

# Modal verbs and selected form explanation

## Key takeaway
Central modal verbs such as may select a following base verb in the construction used by the editing case.

## Summary
Liz Walter's Cambridge Dictionary article supplies a pedagogical account of modal forms. Its limited contribution is the local selection pattern; it does not validate the entire auxiliary system or the editing Practice.

## Publication identity

[Modal verbs – the basics](https://dictionaryblog.cambridge.org/2016/11/23/modal-verbs-the-basics/), Liz Walter, Cambridge Dictionary About Words, dated 23 November 2016.

## Inspected contribution

The article describes modal inflection and the following verb's base form, with an explicit exception for *ought to*. This supports *may deliver* and the central-modal row in the Syntax Model’s auxiliary selection section. The package uses *plain* and *base* for the relevant verb form. Its delivery example is constructed, not quoted from the article.

## Nonclaims

This is a teaching explanation. We do not adopt every prescription, frequency suggestion or generalization in the article. It does not supply full evidence for perfect, progressive or passive selection, dialectal modal combinations, or editing effectiveness. The *ought to* exception prevents extending the base-without-to pattern to every modal-like expression.

# Speech perception cues and categorization

## Key takeaway
An acoustic difference and a perceptual distinction require different evidence.

## Summary
Selected lecture text supports testing perceptual cue use and distinguishing identification from discrimination. It does not provide listener responses for the package's examples.

## Publication and inspection
[Lecture PDF](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/24-915-linguistic-phonetics-fall-2015/1681570d3f89ea52907a8a9ae5956445_MIT24_915F15_lec9.pdf), accessed 2026-10-14; page numbers are one-based. ## Contribution
Pages 3 through 5 motivate cue manipulation and distinguish positive and negative VOT. Pages 9 through 14 separate labeling from discrimination and qualify categorical-perception claims. Pages 22 and 23 identify multiple acoustic cues. The local timing arithmetic is constructed.

## Nonclaims
The underlying experiments were not independently inspected or replicated. No graph values, universal VOT threshold, fixed cue weighting, complete perception theory or learner results are claimed. Source figures and recordings are not redistributed.

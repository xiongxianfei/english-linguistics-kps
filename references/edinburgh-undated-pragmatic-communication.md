# Pragmatics of Linguistic Communication readings

## Key takeaway
Expression meaning, contextual reference and communicative action require separate analyses.

## Summary
Selected Edinburgh teaching pages support the Model's introductory distinctions and its form/force comparison. Adapted source material is attributed below without claiming inspection of the original works.

## Publication and inspection
[Course index](https://pragmatics.ppls.ed.ac.uk/~hannah/plc/index.html), accessed 2026-10-14. Pages are recorded as undated rather than assigning a year from the course calendar.

## Utterances
[Sections 1.1 through 1.3](https://pragmatics.ppls.ed.ac.uk/~hannah/plc/utterances.html) distinguish expression meaning from situated use. The page credits Huang 2007, chapter 1; that book was not separately inspected. The Model avoids equating a sentence type with a particular utterance token.

## Reference
[Section 8.1](https://pragmatics.ppls.ed.ac.uk/~hannah/plc/reference-1intro.html) illustrates speaker-dependent person reference and ambiguous discourse pronouns. The Model retains unresolved candidates rather than presuming context always singles out one referent. Research results cited later on the page are not evidence for our examples.

## Speech acts
[Section 6.1](https://pragmatics.ppls.ed.ac.uk/~hannah/plc/speech-acts-1intro.html) introduces action through language. [Section 6.3](https://pragmatics.ppls.ed.ac.uk/~hannah/plc/speech-acts-3felicity.html) distinguishes locution, illocution, effects and felicity conditions. The Model's promise scenario is an authored application; its interpretation and outcome were not observed.

## Indirect acts
[Section 6.4](https://pragmatics.ppls.ed.ac.uk/~hannah/plc/speech-acts-4indirect.html) distinguishes conventional form-associated force from an intended indirect act. It credits S. Kaufmann's 2008 teaching materials; those originals were not separately inspected.

## Nonclaims
These introductory explanations are not a complete speech-act theory or empirical validation. The Model does not adopt a mandatory literal-first processing sequence or a universal one-to-one mapping from grammatical form to force. Underlying studies, media, original books and universal politeness claims remain outside this inspection.

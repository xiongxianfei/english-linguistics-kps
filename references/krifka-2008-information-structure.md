# Basic notions of information structure

## Key takeaway

Focus alternatives, contextual givenness and aboutness topics answer different explanatory questions; their interaction matters for interpretation and realization.

## Summary

Krifka's 2008 article presents a theoretical account of information organization. Selected passages support distinctions used in the Information Structure Model and bounded connections with Prosody, Syntax and Semantics. The repository's examples and organization are authored applications, not the article's analyses or independent empirical findings.

## Focus and questions

Sections 2–3.6 distinguish shared information from management of the exchange and develop an alternatives-based account of focus. Question–answer congruence, corrective uses and focus-sensitive operators illustrate different contributions. The selected prose also distinguishes packaging from changes to truth conditions and notes interpretive differences between clefts and in-situ focus. The repository's two readings of *only lent* are simplified constructed applications, not a complete operator semantics.

## Givenness and accent

Section 4 separates contextual availability from focus. It discusses anaphoric expressions, deaccentuation and interaction between givenness and accent placement, including given material within a larger focused constituent. This supports keeping given status, focus span and accent location separate; it does not supply recordings or a complete focus-projection derivation for our examples.

## Topics and contrast

Sections 5.1–5.2 distinguish topic/comment from background/focus and grammatical Subject. Contrastive topics can indicate alternative aboutness topics and partial answers. The repository adopts introductory distinctions without claiming the article's full explanatory framework is consensus.

## Nonclaims

No complete article evaluation, underlying study replication, corpus analysis, audio inspection, learner outcome or independent linguistic review is reported. Section 3.7's formal alternatives are not implemented. Construction choice, exhaustive cleft interpretations, fine-grained accessibility and cross-variety prosodic realization need broader evidence. The source is not redistributed, and its support does not automatically validate separate Concepts or Principles.

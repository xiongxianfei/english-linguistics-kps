# Implicature and conversational alternatives

## Key takeaway
Scalar reasoning depends on relevance and speaker knowledge; a stronger negative conclusion does not follow from wording alone.

## Summary
The inspected handout supports a bounded contrast between entailment and cancellable implicature and identifies alternative reasons for not making a stronger statement.

## Publication and inspection
[Handout](https://web.mit.edu/rbhatt/www/24.903/l10.pdf), accessed 2026-10-14. [Course page](https://web.mit.edu/rbhatt/www/24.903/) establishes attribution. Sections 3 and 4 on PDF pages 1 and 2 are the principal contribution; section 5 was inspected as text only.

## Contribution
The Pragmatics Model uses the relevance and knowledge qualifications in section 4. Its printer exchange and stepwise explanation are authored applications, not source examples or observations.

## Nonclaims
This is a teaching account of selected scalar inferences, not a complete treatment of implicature or a verified processing algorithm. The repository does not reproduce its formal proof, claim that every scale behaves identically or infer experimental results.

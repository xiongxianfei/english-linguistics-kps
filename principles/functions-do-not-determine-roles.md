# Grammatical functions do not determine semantic roles

## Key takeaway
A Subject need not be an Agent, and an Object need not be a Patient.

## Summary
A Subject need not be an Agent, and an Object need not be a Patient. The definitions and worked cases below state the intended analysis and its limits. This initial object is provisional; links deepen the explanation but do not replace it.

## Statement

A Subject need not be an Agent, and an Object need not be a Patient.

## Explanation

Grammatical functions organize constituents; semantic roles describe relations to a situation. In alternations the same participant relation can have different grammatical realizations. In psych predicates similar Subject-Object structure can assign roles different from an agentive action.

## Worked contrast

"Maya was given a medal" makes Maya Subject and Recipient. "The noise frightened Ravi" makes the noise Subject and Stimulus/Causer, and Ravi Object and Experiencer. Passive therefore cannot be diagnosed simply by an affected Subject.

## Scope and counterevidence

The broad distinction is supported by the selected passive source passage; the exact Patient/Theme inventory and these constructed analyses still require independent review. Not every alternation preserves all aspects of meaning.

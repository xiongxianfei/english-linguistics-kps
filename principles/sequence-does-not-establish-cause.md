# Temporal sequence does not establish causation

## Key takeaway
An event following another in time does not by itself demonstrate that the earlier event caused it.

## Summary
An event following another in time does not by itself demonstrate that the earlier event caused it. The definitions and worked cases below state the intended analysis and its limits. This initial object is provisional; links deepen the explanation but do not replace it.

## Statement

An event following another in time does not by itself demonstrate that the earlier event caused it.

## Explanation

A temporal relation specifies ordering; causal explanation requires a suitable dependency or mechanism. Discourse may encourage a causal inference through relevance and world knowledge, but the inference needs more than sentence order.

## Worked contrast

"Maya entered. Ravi sneezed" can describe sequence without a causal link. "The road was icy. The car skidded" supports a plausible causal explanation, yet other causes remain possible. Evidence that a person left is not automatically a cause of their departure.

## Scope and counterevidence

The examples illustrate inferential distinctions, not empirical causal findings. Even explicit causal language reports a causal claim; it does not make that claim true.

# Investigate linguistic question

## Key takeaway
Turn a bounded English question into a transparent provisional analysis and a reviewable evidence trail.

## Summary
Turn a bounded English question into a transparent provisional analysis and a reviewable evidence trail. The definitions and worked cases below state the intended analysis and its limits. This initial object is provisional; links deepen the explanation but do not replace it.

## Purpose and prerequisites

This is a proposed research-maintenance Practice for readers using the package, not an English-learning course. The numbered stages give a recommended order; actual prerequisites, evidence availability and review needs determine entry. Earlier stages can be revisited. No use outcomes or learning gains have been recorded.

## Stage1 Define the question

**Goal:** Specify one contrast and the intended variety.

**Why:** A broad topic cannot establish which observation would discriminate competing accounts.

1. **What to do:** Write the expression, context, candidate analyses and current uncertainty.

**What to observe:** Ambiguous targets, missing context and claims imported from teaching shorthand.

**Success signal:** A reader can identify the exact phenomenon and the conditions under which it is being analyzed.

**Next:** Proceed to the representation pass, or narrow the question.

**Procedure**

Choose one contrast such as depend on Leo versus depend Leo in the stated standard variety. Preserve the full original expression. Record whether examples are invented, copied with permission, or collected from speakers. Write at least one alternative hypothesis and what additional evidence would distinguish it. Define terms locally so the next stage is executable without opening a chain of notes.

**Decision points and fallback**

If the problem requires audio, a regional variety or unavailable speaker context, record the missing data instead of inventing them. Narrow the initial claim to what the available material can support.

## Stage2 Separate representations

**Goal:** Keep the relevant linguistic layers distinct.

**Why:** Mistakes often assign a correct label to the wrong span or analytical dimension.

1. **What to do:** Annotate morphology, local syntax, semantic interpretation and contextual contributions separately.

**What to observe:** A pronoun referent confused with a semantic role, or a full NP called a relative clause.

**Success signal:** Each label names a stated object and a declared relationship.

**Next:** Evaluate sources and counterexamples; return here if the representation changes.

**Procedure**

Create a short table with expression span, category, local head, function, role and reference fields as appropriate. Do not force irrelevant fields into every task. For a PP, distinguish the preposition licensing its NP from the verb licensing the complete PP. In a scope problem, write both quantified readings without assuming distinct existential witnesses. State any theoretical commitments alongside the proposed model.

**Decision points and fallback**

When multiple analyses remain possible, keep them side by side. A diagram is a representation of the proposal, not evidence that the proposal is true.

## Stage3 Inspect evidence

**Goal:** Establish what the claim is actually supported by.

**Why:** Sources and judgments have limits that must survive summarization.

1. **What to do:** Read relevant passages and record source identity, locators, access extent and nonclaims.

**What to observe:** Unsupported universals, accent mismatches, or multiple summaries of the same original account.

**Success signal:** The claim has bounded support or remains explicitly provisional.

**Next:** Publish a draft analysis or request review, without inflating its status.

**Procedure**

Use the claim-review Method locally: classify the claim, identify needed evidence, inspect a source passage, compare its scope, and seek a counterexample. A publisher description may establish a book identity but not validate a syntactic analysis. Write your own summary and distinguish direct source statements from inferred explanations. If full text is unavailable, record that fact and keep uninspected claims on the review queue.

**Decision points and fallback**

Do not upload restricted source PDFs or source illustrations. If uncertainty matters to the analysis, narrow the statement or leave both alternatives open rather than finding a decorative citation.

## Stage4 Record the analysis

**Goal:** Produce a reusable object or a bounded correction.

**Why:** A documented interpretation can be compared and revised without reconstructing the whole conversation.

1. **What to do:** Choose the KPS role, explain locally, link actual dependencies and record unresolved questions.

**What to observe:** Evidence status, examples labelled as constructed, and summaries affected by the change.

**Success signal:** The file is usable with links hidden and structurally checked.

**Next:** Request independent review or use the result as a provisional premise with explicit limits.

**Procedure**

Create a Concept for a distinction, Principle for an explanatory relationship, Model for a representation, or Method for an operation. Include the result and counterexample in the file rather than replacing explanation with links. Run structural checks and inspect incoming summaries. Record real checks in a review note; do not turn generated examples into actual Practice outcomes.

**Decision points and fallback**

A structural pass is not scientific approval. Keep status draft and evidence provisional until a documented independent review justifies any stronger label.

# Scope and theory policy

## Key takeaway
The draft states its working analyses and variety limits rather than presenting one framework as universal.

## Summary
The draft states its working analyses and variety limits rather than presenting one framework as universal. The initial broad release remains provisional; supporting links and checks do not constitute independent scientific review.

## Audience and purpose

The primary audience is an independent reader building a scientific model of English. The repository is knowledge-first, not a sequence of graded lessons. Methods and Practices support investigation, review and bounded pronunciation work. The two pronunciation Practices additionally integrate analysis, listening, articulation, production and verification; their learning effectiveness has not been established. They do not turn the knowledge base into a graded course or certify a reader's competence.

## Working syntax

The package uses NP terminology for nominal phrases and a descriptive Subject/Predicate analysis for simple clauses. CP/TP and DP accounts are recognized alternatives. Relative clauses, adjunct clauses and nonfinite complements are identified using explicit spans and local dependencies. The treatment of subordinators and auxiliaries is stated where relevant, not asserted as a universal taxonomy.

## Working semantics

Agent, Patient, Theme, Recipient, Experiencer and Stimulus are working role labels. Inventories overlap and vary between theories. Scope formulas illustrate classical finite-domain reasoning; natural-language scope accessibility requires further evidence. Events may be entities in a broad ontology. Tense/aspect diagrams are examples, not exhaustive semantics. Perfect does not automatically mean completion.

## Working sound conventions

**General American English is the primary reference variety for new pronunciation examples.** The [pronunciation policy](PRONUNCIATION.md) defines broad phonemic versus narrow phonetic notation, rhoticity, the conventional omission of phonemic vowel length marks, and the roles of Sounds American, toPhonetics and the official interactive IPA chart. Existing British-style transcriptions are legacy accent-marked examples, not silently reclassified as American. An American broad /i/ is not a literal claim of a constant cardinal phone, and a broad /r/ need not be a trilled [r]. Accent and notation differences require explicit labels and should not be converted by blind text replacement.

Neither the terms 'General American' nor 'standard' identifies a single invariant speaker or devalues other dialects. Aspiration depends on stress and syllable context, not simply word position. Narrow token-specific claims require actual recorded or measured evidence; none is claimed here.

## Boundaries

This release does not exhaust sociolinguistics, language acquisition, diachrony, corpus linguistics, psycholinguistic processing or every competing formal theory. The ten subsystem Models provide foundational coverage, not a complete inventory or a compulsory reading order.

## Treatment of prior material

The learning conversation supplied candidate distinctions and constructed examples. It is not cited as an external scientific authority. Earlier generated posters are excluded because image text, IPA and simplified claims require a separate audit. Private learning mistakes and personal context are not published as user data.

# English pragmatics system

This Model explains thirteen connected Pragmatics topics, from sentence and speaker meaning through context, reference, presupposition, implicature and speech acts to common ground and the Semantics interface. It compares three kinds of inference, works through Gricean reasoning and analyzes a constructed conversation. Topic sections supply the essential distinctions locally; Concepts deepen definitions and neighboring Models explain interfaces. Selected teaching sources support bounded claims. The analysis remains provisional and does not establish a speaker's actual intention or a learner's competence.

Given an utterance and its available context, explain a plausible interpretation and what would change it. Familiarity with English sentences is sufficient to begin; the technical terms are introduced below. Missing context remains an uncertainty rather than permission to invent an intention.

## Representation

Represent an interpretation as a set of connected claims:

| Part | Record | Connection |
|---|---|---|
| Expression | Wording, construction and selected senses | Constrains possible interpretations |
| Occasion | Speaker, addressee, time, place and prior turns | Supplies contextual values and candidate referents |
| Shared background | What participants treat as accepted | Supports reference and presupposition satisfaction |
| Current issue | The question or purpose being addressed | Makes some alternatives and inferences relevant |
| Interpretation | Resolved content, background and additional inferences | Supports a proposed communicative action |
| Uptake | Acceptance, challenge, clarification or response, if observed | Can revise the next conversational context |

This is an analytical representation, not a claim that comprehension follows six successive stages. Context constrains interpretation, and an interpreted contribution can change context.

## Essential distinctions
An **utterance** is a particular use of an expression. **Conventional meaning** comes from words and constructions. **Contextual resolution** supplies values required on that occasion. **Presupposition** concerns background treated as already available. **Implicature** concerns additional communicated content inferred under conversational assumptions. **Speech-act force** concerns the action performed or attempted. **Uptake** concerns the recipient's response. These distinctions interact; none should be collapsed into a single label such as “implied meaning.”

All examples below are authored, constructed cases. They illustrate an analysis; they are not recordings or elicited judgments.

## Sentence meaning vs speaker meaning
Sentence meaning concerns the conventional contribution of an expression's words and structure. Speaker meaning concerns what someone intends to communicate by using it. An utterance brings an expression into a particular situation. A hearer's interpretation is evidence about neither intention nor truth by itself.

*The printer is empty* can report a machine's condition. Said to a colleague holding paper during a printing task, it might also prompt replenishment. That proposed request needs contextual support; the sentence does not mean “refill the printer” on every use. In a maintenance report, no request need be intended. The [Edinburgh utterance reading](../references/edinburgh-undated-pragmatic-communication.md#utterances) supports the expression/use distinction.

## Context
For this Model, context includes participants, physical surroundings, time, preceding discourse, the current issue and assumptions treated as shared. A fact known only to the analyst must not silently become a participant's knowledge.

For *It is ready now*, ask which item *it* identifies, what counts as ready for the current purpose, and when the utterance occurs. In a print room, readiness might concern a printer or a report. Without the preceding exchange, leave both candidates open. Context narrows an interpretation; it does not authorize arbitrary replacement of lexical meaning. [Fox and Menendez-Benito](../references/fox-menendez-benito-2006-pragmatics.md) distinguish contextual dependence from conversational inference.

## Deixis
**Deixis** anchors expressions to the occasion of use: person (*I*, *you*), place (*here*, *there*) and time (*now*, *tomorrow*). The anchor can depend on whether wording is directly quoted, reported or used in a narrative perspective.

If Maya says *I will check it tomorrow* on Monday, ordinary direct speech identifies *I* with Maya and *tomorrow* with Tuesday. When Leo says the same words on Tuesday, the anchors change. Keep the utterance occasion separate from the date on which an analyst reads its transcript. The person-anchoring contrast has selected [Edinburgh reference support](../references/edinburgh-undated-pragmatic-communication.md#reference); the time and quotation examples are local extensions requiring fuller source review.

## Reference in context
Reference analysis asks which entity an expression picks out in a particular use. Deictic reference can use the situation; **anaphoric** reference depends on discourse. They can interact, and not every pronoun has a uniquely recoverable referent.

Compare *Pass that folder*, accompanied by a clear gesture, with *A folder arrived. It contains the invoice*. The first uses a demonstrated object; the second links *it* to an introduced referent. In *Maya put a folder beside a notebook. It was blue*, both objects remain candidates without more evidence. A recent mention alone does not decide.

Record candidates, linguistic constraints and the evidence favoring one interpretation. The [Edinburgh reading](../references/edinburgh-undated-pragmatic-communication.md#reference) supports ambiguity and discourse dependence; [Gibson's selected slides](../references/gibson-2017-pragmatic-reference.md) identify interlocutor perspective as relevant to reference.

## Presupposition
A presupposition is background associated with an expression or construction under an analysis. **Projection** is the tendency for such background to remain relevant inside a larger expression. **Accommodation** supplies previously unstated background; a hearer may instead challenge it.

*Maya stopped checking the printer* normally backgrounds earlier checking. *Maya did not stop checking the printer* can retain that background while denying cessation. But *If Maya used to check it, she has now stopped* illustrates why embedding and local context matter: the prior activity is being considered conditionally, not simply asserted as fact.

Compare *I did not know she had been checking it* with *She never checked it*. The first can accommodate the background; the second challenges it. [Fox and Menendez-Benito's notes](../references/fox-menendez-benito-2006-presupposition.md) support background, projection and accommodation, without making projection an exceptionless test.

## Entailment vs presupposition vs implicature
An **entailment** holds when the source proposition cannot be true and the consequence false under the same interpretation and assumptions. Compare three analyses before labeling something “implied”:

| Kind | Constructed pair | What the comparison shows |
|---|---|---|
| Entailment | *Maya checked P1 and P2* entails *Maya checked P1* | Denying the consequence contradicts the conjunction on the stated reading |
| Presupposition | *Maya stopped checking P1* backgrounds earlier checking | The background can survive negation; it can also be challenged or locally satisfied |
| Conversational implicature | *Maya checked some printers* may suggest not all | *In fact, she checked all of them* can cancel the suggestion without denying the existential claim |

These are different relations, not necessarily three disjoint sets of propositions. State a reading before testing it. Negation, embedding, cancellation and background challenge provide converging diagnostics, not a universal classifier. The [Semantics Model](english-semantics-system.md) explains truth-conditional inference. [Bhatt](../references/bhatt-2003-implicature.md) supports the cancellability contrast; the presupposition notes support context-sensitive projection.

## Conversational implicature
A conversational implicature is communicated content inferred from the utterance and assumptions about the exchange rather than required by its basic truth conditions. An arbitrary association in a listener's mind is not enough.

Asked *Did you inspect all the printers?*, a technician's *I inspected some of them* can suggest not all. Asked *Did any inspection happen?*, the same reply may simply answer the immediate concern. If the technician adds *I cannot remember whether I finished the rest*, uncertainty is another possibility. Distinguish a stronger negative conclusion from lack of evidence for the stronger alternative.

The mechanism connects relevant alternatives, the speaker's apparent knowledge and the conversational purpose. The next section makes those assumptions visible. This is a bounded scalar example, not a complete theory of implicature; [Bhatt's handout](../references/bhatt-2003-implicature.md) supplies selected support.

## Gricean reasoning
Gricean analysis uses expectations about **Quantity** (appropriate informativeness), **Quality** (truthfulness and evidential support), **Relation** (relevance) and **Manner** (clear expression). These are interpretive expectations, not commands that every speaker obeys. [Gibson's slides](../references/gibson-2017-pragmatic-reference.md) introduce the four labels.

For the technician's reply, a possible derivation is:

1. *All* would answer the manager's question more strongly than existential *some*.
2. Assume the stronger alternative is relevant and the technician would use it if warranted.
3. Its absence can suggest that the technician cannot endorse *all*.
4. Only with an additional knowledge assumption can this support the stronger conclusion that the technician believes *not all*.

A technician who saw only part of a colleague's work may lack complete information. That countercase blocks step 4. [Bhatt's derivation](../references/bhatt-2003-implicature.md) explicitly distinguishes irrelevance, ignorance and a negative belief. Overtly exploiting an expectation, such as recognizable irony, also differs from covert deception; fuller support for those cases remains pending in the [Gricean reasoning Concept](../concepts/gricean-reasoning.md).

## Speech acts
A speech act is an action performed or attempted through communication: asserting, asking, requesting, promising or apologizing, for example. Distinguish the **locution** (meaningful expression used), **illocutionary force** (the act) and **perlocutionary effect** (what it brings about).

*I will inspect P2 before lunch*, in response to an explicit request for a commitment, may be a promise. The future-oriented sentence alone does not prove that force; a schedule prediction is another use. A promise can be recognized without being fulfilled. Felicity concerns conditions for the act, such as the relevant commitment and sincerity, rather than grammatical well-formedness alone. [Edinburgh section 6.3](../references/edinburgh-undated-pragmatic-communication.md#speech-acts) supplies the terminology and selected conditions.

## Direct vs indirect speech acts
A direct use aligns with the force conventionally associated with its form in the adopted account; an indirect use conveys an additional or different act. Compare *Please pass that folder* with *Could you pass that folder?* in a document-sharing task. The interrogative can function as a request. In an assessment of physical ability, an ability question remains plausible.

| Interpretation field | Folder example |
|---|---|
| Clause and conventional contribution | Interrogative modal construction about passing |
| Contextual values | Addressee and demonstrated folder |
| Proposed force and evidence | Request, supported by the shared document task |
| Possible effect | Recipient passes the folder; not observed here |
| Alternative | Inquiry about ability in another situation |

The request is not a permanent lexical meaning of *could*. Conventionalized indirect forms also make a rigid literal-then-inferred processing sequence questionable; this worksheet does not assert one. [Edinburgh section 6.4](../references/edinburgh-undated-pragmatic-communication.md#indirect-acts) supports the form/force distinction, not a universal politeness ranking.

## Common ground
Common ground is what participants treat as mutually accepted for their exchange. It differs from a person's private information and from everything objectively true. A proposal can be accepted, challenged or misunderstood; an analyst must track whose commitments are represented.

Suppose Maya privately knows that P2 is broken but Leo has not been told. That fact cannot yet explain Leo's interpretation as shared background. Maya's *P2 has failed again* may invite Leo to accommodate an earlier failure. *Again? I thought this was its first fault* challenges that assumption. [Gibson](../references/gibson-2017-pragmatic-reference.md) distinguishes shared and privileged information; the [presupposition notes](../references/fox-menendez-benito-2006-presupposition.md) connect background requirements and accommodation. Shared acceptance in this working account is not a guarantee of truth.

## Information structure and discourse context
**Information structure** concerns how an utterance organizes content relative to discourse: what it is about (**topic**), which alternatives matter (**focus**) and what is treated as available (**givenness**). A **question under discussion**, or QUD, is the current issue an exchange addresses. These distinctions are not interchangeable.

Compare *Who checked P1? — [Maya] checked P1* with *Which printer did Maya check? — Maya checked [P1]*. The first contrast selects a person; the second selects a printer. Brackets mark proposed focus spans, not measured prominence or audio. Naming P1 earlier can make its referent available without settling the current question.

The [Information Structure Model](english-information-structure-system.md) explains the representation more fully. Pragmatics uses the current issue to assess relevance; Discourse tracks its development across turns; Prosody can cue packaging without uniquely determining it. [Roberts](../references/roberts-2012-information-structure.md) supports only the broad QUD/focus proposal at abstract level. This comparison remains an authored illustration, not a verified focus analysis.

## Semantics Pragmatics interface
Semantics constrains interpretation through conventional contributions. Pragmatics relates expressions to use, background and inferred communication. Context sensitivity alone does not decide the boundary: semantic theories can take contexts as arguments.

| Expression | Conventional constraint | Context or inference |
|---|---|---|
| *I* | Speaker-related interpretation rule | Maya in this direct utterance |
| *here* | Contextually anchored place | The desk or room, depending on the occasion |
| *some* | Existential contribution in this example | Possible not-all inference under stated assumptions |
| *stop* | Change-of-state contribution with background | Prior activity accommodated, satisfied or challenged |

Resolving *I* and inferring *not all* are different operations. Neither permits arbitrary lexical reinterpretation. The counterpart is in [Semantics](english-semantics-system.md#interface-with-pragmatics). [Fox and Menendez-Benito's introduction](../references/fox-menendez-benito-2006-pragmatics.md) motivates the distinctions while leaving theoretical boundaries open. Presupposition particularly connects conventional triggers with discourse behavior.

## Worked analysis
Constructed setting: Maya and Leo share a list containing P1 and P2. Both know Maya was assigned their inspection. No inspection result is initially shared.

> Leo: Did you inspect all the printers?
>
> Maya: I inspected some of them.
>
> Leo: So P2 still needs checking?
>
> Maya: No, I checked both. P2 has stopped jamming.
>
> Leo: I did not know it had been jamming. Could you put that folder here?
>
> Maya: The blue one?
>
> Leo: Yes, beside me.
>
> Maya: I will move it after I finish this note.

The constructed scene includes Leo indicating a folder and the desk beside him; there is no recording.

| Contribution | Analysis and limit |
|---|---|
| *all the printers* | The shared list restricts the relevant set to P1 and P2 |
| *I*, *them* | Speaker anchoring identifies Maya; the prior question supplies the printer referents |
| *some* | Existential inspection content; a possible not-all inference given the QUD |
| *So P2…?* | Leo's proposed conclusion reveals an interpretation, but *some* alone identifies no particular unchecked printer |
| *I checked both* | Cancels not-all and, on the shared two-item domain, entails that Maya checked P1 and P2 |
| *stopped jamming* | Backgrounds prior jamming while reporting cessation; this does not establish that P2 has no other fault |
| *I did not know…* | Acknowledges previously unavailable background rather than proving it was shared all along |
| *Could you…that…here* | Proposed request with demonstrative and spatial anchoring; folder identity still needs clarification |
| *The blue one? — Yes…* | A clarification exchange narrows reference; the actual physical action is not reported |
| *I will move it…* | A possible commitment with a timing condition, not evidence of completed compliance |

This one conversation joins meaning, context, reference, inference, action and update without treating any one component as sufficient. Leo's first conclusion is corrigible. Its correction shows why a Model should retain alternatives instead of storing every initial inference as fact.

## Interfaces
Pragmatics draws on conventional meaning from [Semantics](#semantics-pragmatics-interface), follows proposals and uptake in [Discourse](#interface-with-discourse), and uses the current issue and packaging from [Information Structure](#information-structure-and-discourse-context). Prosodic cues can constrain interpretations, but no tone universally identifies one intention. These are connections within the subject, not additional Model files.

### Interface with Discourse
Represent the prior context, proposed contribution, candidate referents and background, then possible next states. An assertion does not automatically become accepted or true. A discourse referent can be available for discussion without an independently established real-world object.

After *A courier left a parcel*, *It was damaged* maintains reference to the parcel, while *No courier came today* challenges the earlier contribution. Keep the proposal and uptake separate. Compare the counterpart in [Discourse](english-discourse-system.md#interface-with-pragmatics).

This update vocabulary is a local working representation, not a complete dynamic semantics or dialogue algorithm. Quotation, hypotheticals and misinformation require explicit scope. The [presupposition notes](../references/fox-menendez-benito-2006-presupposition.md) offer selected context-update motivation, not evidence for every possible response in this constructed parcel case.

## Evaluation and counterevidence
Hold wording fixed while varying one contextual condition. Check whether the proposed referent, inference or act changes for a stated reason. An ability-assessment context can weaken a request interpretation; an uninformed speaker can block the strong not-all inference. Negation and embedding probe background behavior; cancellation probes an added inference. A coherent alternative should remain available when evidence does not decide.

For the integrated case, explain why Leo cannot infer specifically that P2 was unchecked, why *both* corrects his interpretation, and why the folder request does not prove compliance. An analysis that merges those distinctions needs revision even if all its terminology is present.

## Assumptions and limits
This introductory Model does not exhaust Pragmatics. Presupposition theories, scalar inference mechanisms and the semantic/pragmatic boundary remain contested. Politeness, irony, metaphor, cross-cultural interaction and processing require fuller treatment and evidence. The source selection mixes graduate and introductory teaching accounts; it is not a single validated processing theory. No speaker intentions, corpus frequencies, acoustic patterns or learner outcomes were observed here.

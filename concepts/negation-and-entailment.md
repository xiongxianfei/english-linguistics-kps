# Negation and entailment

## Key takeaway
Under a stated classical interpretation, P entails Q if every model making P true makes Q true.

## Summary

## Definition

Under a stated classical interpretation, P entails Q if every model making P true makes Q true. Negation reverses the truth value of its scope. Entailment, presupposition and conversational implicature are distinct relations, though real examples can carry more than one.

## Worked example

"Maya owns three books" entails that she owns at least one book under both exactly-three and at-least-three numeral readings. "Not every reader agreed" has NOT > EVERY. "Every reader failed to agree" has EVERY > NOT. With at least one reader, the latter entails the former, but not conversely.

## Distinctions

No reader agreed does not entail Not every reader agreed if the relevant reader set is empty in classical logic. A cancellation test must preserve the intended meanings and domain rather than silently change them.

## Scope and limits

Presupposition failure, metalinguistic negation and nonclassical logics complicate the simple truth-table picture. The empty-domain qualification is essential when stating logical-strength claims.

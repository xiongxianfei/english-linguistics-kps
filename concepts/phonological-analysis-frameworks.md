# Phonological analysis frameworks

## Key takeaway
A phonological analysis depends on representational and theoretical choices, so the same pattern may receive competing feature, rule, constraint or usage-based accounts.

## Summary
Phonological frameworks provide different ways to represent contrasts, alternations and well-formedness. KPS records the chosen assumptions locally instead of presenting one framework as the only analysis.

## Definition
A **phonological analysis framework** specifies the units, representations and explanatory mechanisms used to describe sound patterns. Frameworks may differ over underlying representations, feature organization, rule ordering, constraints, probabilistic knowledge and the relation between lexical storage and productive generalization.

## Distinctions
An observed distribution is evidence to explain; it is not itself a commitment to one theory. A phonemic transcription convention also does not uniquely determine a full theoretical analysis. Descriptive adequacy, learnability, typological fit and processing plausibility are separate evaluation dimensions.

## Worked example
The English plural alternation can be described with three listed surface exponents or with one abstract exponent plus phonological adjustments. Those analyses may agree on many predictions while differing in representation and mechanism.

## Scope and limits
This foundation does not teach the formal machinery of every framework or rank them globally. Each Model should state which assumptions it adopts and what counterevidence would matter.

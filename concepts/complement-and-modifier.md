# Complement and modifier

## Definition

A complement is licensed by the relevant head or construction in a closer selectional relationship; a modifier contributes a less lexically restricted elaboration. These are dependency relations, not word classes, and omission by itself is not a complete diagnostic.

## Worked example

In "Maya put the case under the desk", the locative PP fills a slot in placement PUT. In "Maya slept under the desk", the same PP adds a location circumstance. In "Maya ate lunch", lunch is an Object complement even though "Maya ate" is possible in suitable context.

## Distinctions

PP does not mean modifier. Complement does not mean obligatory word. In "proud of the team", the PP can be an adjective complement; inside that PP the NP is a complement of OF.

## Scope and limits

The distinction has borderline cases. Noun complements, manner phrases and constructionally licensed dependents require explicit analysis rather than a single deletion test. Different frameworks delimit the term complement differently.

# Semantic role

## Key takeaway
A semantic role characterizes a participant's relationship to a predicate or situation.

## Summary
A semantic role characterizes a participant's relationship to a predicate or situation. The definitions and worked cases below state the intended analysis and its limits. This initial object is provisional; links deepen the explanation but do not replace it.

## Definition

A semantic role characterizes a participant's relationship to a predicate or situation. Agent, Patient, Theme, Recipient, Experiencer and Stimulus form a useful introductory inventory, but are not grammatical functions or permanent labels on entities.

## Worked example

In "Maya opened the box", Maya is Agent and the box Patient. In "The box was opened by Maya", the box becomes Subject while remaining Patient. In "Maya was given a prize", Maya is Subject and Recipient, not Patient merely because the clause is passive.

## Distinctions

Theme often labels something moved or transferred; Patient emphasizes affectedness or change; Recipient receives something. "The noise frightened Ravi" has a Stimulus/Causer Subject and Experiencer Object.

## Scope and limits

Role inventories and boundaries differ. Passive need not contain an overt agent phrase, and not every event is agentive. Broad Theme labels in some sources differ from the finer Patient distinction used here.

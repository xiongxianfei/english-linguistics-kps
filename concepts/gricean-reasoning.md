# Gricean reasoning

## Definition

Gricean analysis models conversational expectations involving informativeness, truthfulness or support, relevance and clarity. The familiar Quantity, Quality, Relation and Manner labels are interpretive principles rather than rules that every actual speaker always follows.

## Worked example

When asked whether all files were checked, "Some were checked" invites comparison with a stronger alternative. A not-all conclusion requires assumptions about relevance and the speaker's knowledge; lack of complete information can block it. When asked where Ravi is, "His bicycle is outside" supplies indirect evidence. The inference depends on assuming the response bears on the conversational issue.

## Distinctions

Flouting is an overt departure that can invite another interpretation, as in recognizable irony; covert misleading is not the same operation. Cooperation does not mean friendship or constant honesty.

## Scope and limits

This is a classical explanatory framework, not an experimentally validated algorithm implemented by this repository. Competing pragmatic theories and culturally variable communicative norms remain relevant.

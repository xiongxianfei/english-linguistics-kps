# Phrase and clause

## Key takeaway
A phrase is a syntactic unit organized as a phrase category; it may contain one word or many.

## Summary
A phrase is a syntactic unit organized as a phrase category; it may contain one word or many. The definitions and worked cases below state the intended analysis and its limits. This initial object is provisional; links deepen the explanation but do not replace it.

## Definition

A phrase is a syntactic unit organized as a phrase category; it may contain one word or many. A clause organizes predication and may contain an overt subject, an understood subject, auxiliaries and embedded clauses. Surface verb counts do not uniquely determine clause counts.

## Worked example

"The very old building" is an NP. "The building collapsed" is a finite clause. In "Anna wants to leave", the infinitival complement "to leave" is analyzed here as a nonfinite clause with an understood subject. In "Anna has been waiting", three verb forms belong to one auxiliary chain.

## Distinctions

Clause is a syntactic category, whereas proposition is a semantic notion. Questions, imperatives and fragments prevent a universal equation of clause with asserted proposition. A sentence can consist of or contain multiple clauses.

## Scope and limits

This package uses a descriptive clause model and recognizes nonfinite clauses. Some theories project clauses as TP or CP and analyze auxiliary complements differently; those choices must be stated when comparing counts.

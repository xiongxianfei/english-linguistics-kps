# Tense and aspect

## Key takeaway
Tense relates temporal reference to an evaluation context; aspect concerns temporal perspective and situation structure.

## Summary

## Definition

Tense relates temporal reference to an evaluation context; aspect concerns temporal perspective and situation structure. The perfect is treated here as a separate construction relating a prior situation or prior interval to a reference time, not as a synonym for completion.

## Worked example

In "Maya had finished before Ravi arrived", the finishing precedes a past reference time: E < R < S is a useful simplified diagram. "Maya has lived here for years" can describe continued residence. "Maya was writing a report" views an unfolding interval without entailing report completion.

## Distinctions

Past participle does not mean past tense. Perfect does not mean ended. For continuing perfects, not every part of the situation must be strictly earlier than R. English will is a modal auxiliary construction rather than an inflected future form parallel to worked.

## Scope and limits

Tense and aspect analyses differ, including classifications of perfect and future constructions. A timeline for a bounded event must not be silently generalized to all states, habituals and continuing perfects.

# Phonotactics

## Key takeaway
Phonotactics concerns restrictions and preferences on segment distributions and combinations in a language or variety.

## Summary
Phonotactics concerns restrictions and preferences on segment distributions and combinations in a language or variety. The definitions and worked cases below state the intended analysis and its limits. This initial object is provisional; links deepen the explanation but do not replace it.

## Definition

Phonotactics concerns restrictions and preferences on segment distributions and combinations in a language or variety. A physically producible sequence need not be licensed in a native lexical pattern. Phonotactic possibility is distinct from lexical attestation.

## Worked example

English permits /pl/ in an onset and /m/ in a coda, making a hypothetical /plɪm/ structurally plausible regardless of whether a dialect has such a lexical item. Native word-initial /ŋl/ in /ŋlɪp/ is disfavored or excluded; its final /p/ is not the problem.

## Distinctions

Simply dividing a sequence into onset, nucleus and coda does not show those combinations are permitted. The presence of /ŋ/ in sing does not license it in every position.

## Scope and limits

Loanwords, proper names, dialect variation and marginal patterns complicate categorical statements. This draft uses ordinary native-English examples and does not assert that an invented string has never occurred anywhere.

# Vowel articulation

## Summary

## Definition

Vowel descriptions commonly use height, backness and lip rounding. English lexical vowels can be mapped to approximate IPA qualities, but a broad phonemic symbol should not be mistaken for an exact cardinal-vowel target across accents.

## Worked example

Working labels: /iː/ close front unrounded; /uː/ close back rounded; /æ/ near-open front unrounded; /ə/ central mid-like and usually neutral or unrounded. English schwa is strongly associated with unstressed syllables in the system used here.

## Distinctions

Low does not mean rounded. Front does not logically entail unrounded across all languages. Contemporary English GOOSE vowels may be fronted, and vowel duration is not determined solely by the length mark.

## Scope and limits

Exact qualities, diphthongization and reduction vary by accent and context. No acoustic measurement or judgment of a user's pronunciation is claimed. The table is a simplified reference, not an accent prescription.

# Derivation

## Key takeaway
Derivation forms a related lexeme using a morphological pattern.

## Summary
Derivation forms a related lexeme using a morphological pattern. The definitions and worked cases below state the intended analysis and its limits. This initial object is provisional; links deepen the explanation but do not replace it.

## Definition

Derivation forms a related lexeme using a morphological pattern. It may change lexical category, preserve it, or produce a conventional meaning not fully predictable from the pieces. Productivity and lexicalization are separate questions.

## Worked example

TEACH to TEACHER changes Verb to Noun through -er. HAPPY to UNHAPPY preserves Adjective status. UNHAPPY to unhappier then realizes comparative inflection, so the hierarchy is [[un-happy]-er], with spelling adjustment y to i.

## Distinctions

Category change is evidence for some derivations but is not a necessary condition. A new syntactic use can also involve conversion without an overt affix.

## Scope and limits

The examples are transparent teaching cases. Not every apparent prefix or suffix can be stripped to recover a current English base, and no general rule predicts every conventional meaning.

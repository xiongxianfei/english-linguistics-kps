# Orthography phonology and phonetics

## Key takeaway
Orthography describes writing; phonology describes language-specific sound organization; phonetics describes speech production, signals and perception.

## Definition

Orthography describes writing; phonology describes language-specific sound organization; phonetics describes speech production, signals and perception. Conventional angle brackets, slashes and square brackets help keep these representational levels separate.

## Worked example

For pin, <pin> names the spelling, /pɪn/ a broad phonemic representation, and [pʰɪn] a possible more detailed phonetic transcription. The aspiration symbol ʰ is not the ordinary sequence ph. A transcription is an analysis of selected detail, not a recording of the complete sound wave.

## Distinctions

Broad versus narrow is a scale of detail, not exactly the same distinction as phonemic versus phonetic. A phonetic transcription can itself be relatively broad. Orthographic <th> can represent /θ/ or /ð/ in English.

## Scope and limits

English accents differ in their inventories and realizations. The official IPA supplies symbols but does not determine one universal phonemic analysis of English or warrant an exact transcription without a recording.

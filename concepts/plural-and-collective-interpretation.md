# Plural and collective interpretation

## Key takeaway
Plural expressions can support distributive, collective and cumulative interpretations, so grammatical plurality does not uniquely determine how a predicate applies to participants.

## Summary
Plural interpretation concerns how predicates and quantifiers apply to more than one individual. Context, lexical meaning and construction can favor different readings without changing the overt plural marking.

## Definition
A **distributive** interpretation applies a predicate to relevant individuals separately; a **collective** interpretation applies it to a group acting or being characterized together. Cumulative readings distribute several participants across several events or objects without requiring one-to-one pairing.

## Distinctions
Morphological plural marking identifies a word-form feature, not a complete semantic analysis. A collective noun is not the same as an ordinary plural NP. Scope interactions can introduce further ambiguity.

## Worked example
"The students lifted the piano" naturally permits a collective reading. "The students sneezed" favors individual events. "Three students read two articles" can describe several distribution patterns depending on context and quantifier scope.

## Scope and limits
Formal treatments differ over groups, sums, events and distributivity operators. The examples are constructed and do not establish one universal logical representation.

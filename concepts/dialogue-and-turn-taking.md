# Dialogue and turn taking

## Key takeaway
Dialogue is jointly organized through turns, responses and repair, so an utterance's contribution depends partly on its position in an interaction.

## Summary
Dialogue and turn taking describe how participants coordinate speaking, responding and repairing misunderstandings. The organization is not reducible to punctuation or a fixed alternation of speakers. This initial English-focused account is provisional and does not claim one universal conversation system.

## Definition
A **turn** is a participant's locally bounded contribution to an interaction. Turn taking concerns how participants project possible completion, select or self-select a next speaker, overlap, pause and repair trouble. A response is interpreted relative to the action and expectations established by preceding turns.

## Distinctions
Turn boundaries are not identical to sentences: a turn can contain several clauses, and a syntactically incomplete expression can function as a complete response. Dialogue structure is also not the same as discourse relation structure, although the two interact. Silence may be meaningful, but its interpretation is context-sensitive.

## Worked example
In "Can you close the window?" followed by "Sure", the second turn can function as acceptance rather than merely a comment on ability. If the response is "Which window?", it initiates repair by requesting information needed to complete the action.

## Scope and limits
Actual interaction depends on setting, participants, modality and social norms. Transcripts omit timing, gaze and other conduct unless those observations are explicitly recorded. The examples here are constructed and do not establish frequency or universal sequencing rules.

# Compounding

## Key takeaway
Compounding combines lexical bases into a larger lexical expression.

## Summary
Compounding combines lexical bases into a larger lexical expression. The definitions and worked cases below state the intended analysis and its limits. This initial object is provisional; links deepen the explanation but do not replace it.

## Definition

Compounding combines lexical bases into a larger lexical expression. English orthographic spacing is not a reliable sole diagnostic: a compound may be written solid, hyphenated or separately. Internal morphological structure and whole-expression meaning require separate analysis.

## Worked example

For schoolteachers, a useful hierarchy is `[[school [teach-er]]-s]`. Derivation makes teacher; compounding makes schoolteacher; inflection makes schoolteachers. A schoolteacher is a kind of teacher, supporting teacher as the compound's semantic head in this example.

## Distinctions

Coffee cup can be a compound although it contains a space. Greenhouse has an identifiable internal structure but a conventional plant-growing sense; that sense is not equivalent to green-painted house.

## Scope and limits

Compound diagnostics and headedness vary. Not all English compounds are semantically endocentric, and stress alone is not an infallible test separating compounds from phrases.

# Politeness and social meaning

## Key takeaway
Utterance design can manage social relationships and identities, but politeness and social meaning depend on community norms, activity and participant interpretation rather than fixed word lists.

## Summary
Social pragmatics examines how linguistic choices index stance, respect, distance, solidarity and identity. An indirect form can serve politeness in one context and avoidance, irony or hostility in another.

## Definition
**Politeness** concerns communicative practices used to manage interpersonal expectations and social relations. **Social meaning** concerns the social stances, personae and group associations that linguistic forms can evoke in situated use.

## Distinctions
Grammatical mood is not equivalent to politeness. Indirectness is not always more polite, and a conventional honorific does not guarantee a respectful interaction. Speaker intention, conventional association and hearer uptake should be represented separately.

## Worked example
"Close the door" and "Could you close the door?" can perform related requests, but their force and social effect depend on authority, urgency, tone, relationship and setting. A deliberately formal lexical choice can signal distance, professionalism or humor.

## Scope and limits
Norms vary across communities, languages, generations and situations. The constructed examples do not justify universal rankings or stereotypes about groups.

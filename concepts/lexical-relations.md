# Lexical relations

## Key takeaway
Lexical items and senses can stand in relations such as hyponymy, incompatibility, synonymy and opposition, but these relations apply to specified senses rather than spellings in isolation.

## Summary
Lexical relations organize parts of the lexicon by meaning and use. They support generalization and inference, but apparent synonyms commonly differ in register, collocation, connotation or constructional behavior.

## Definition
A **lexical relation** is a conventional relation among lexical senses. A hyponym denotes a more specific category relative to a broader term; oppositions may be complementary, gradable or relational; synonymy concerns substantial similarity of sense rather than guaranteed interchangeability in every context.

## Distinctions
A word form with several senses can participate in different relations for each sense. Lexical relations are not the same as discourse associations or accidental co-occurrence. Distributional similarity can provide evidence, but it does not by itself establish identical meaning.

## Worked example
In one ordinary use, *sparrow* is a hyponym of *bird*. *Hot* and *cold* form a gradable opposition, while *buy* and *sell* describe related perspectives on a transaction. *Begin* and *start* overlap, but their constructions and collocations are not identical in every case.

## Scope and limits
Relation inventories differ among theories and lexical resources. Dialect, register, specialist terminology and historical change can alter judgments. The examples are constructed and require corpus and dictionary checking for detailed usage claims.

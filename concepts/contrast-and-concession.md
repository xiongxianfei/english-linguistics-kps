# Contrast and concession

## Key takeaway
Contrast presents a relevant difference or opposition between discourse units.

## Summary
Contrast presents a relevant difference or opposition between discourse units. The definitions and worked cases below state the intended analysis and its limits. This initial object is provisional; links deepen the explanation but do not replace it.

## Definition

Contrast presents a relevant difference or opposition between discourse units. Concession additionally invokes an expectation that is not fulfilled. The expectation is usually defeasible rather than a logical implication.

## Worked example

"Maya likes tea, but Ravi likes coffee" contrasts preferences. "Maya was exhausted, but she continued working" defeats an expectation of stopping or resting. Both exhaustion and continued work can be true, so the second sentence does not assert P and not-P.

## Distinctions

Contrast need not compare different people: one person can prefer tea but dislike coffee. But does not mechanically encode one single relation in every context, and although is not a coordinating conjunction in our working syntax.

## Scope and limits

Exact distinctions among concessive contrast, correction and denial of expectation vary across frameworks. No universal two-sense lexical analysis of BUT is assumed.

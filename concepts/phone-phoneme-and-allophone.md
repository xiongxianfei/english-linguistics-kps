# Phone phoneme and allophone

## Key takeaway
A phoneme is an abstract contrastive category in a language analysis.

## Summary
A phoneme is an abstract contrastive category in a language analysis. The definitions and worked cases below state the intended analysis and its limits. This initial object is provisional; links deepen the explanation but do not replace it.

## Definition

A phoneme is an abstract contrastive category in a language analysis. A phone is a phonetic segment type or realization. An allophone is a phonetic variant related to a phoneme. Allophone status therefore depends on a phonological analysis, not just physical similarity.

## Worked example

English pin and bin support a /p/-/b/ contrast. A typical strongly stressed syllable-initial /p/ in pin is aspirated [pʰ], while the /p/ after /s/ in spin is normally unaspirated [p]. Aspiration differences here need not distinguish lexical identity.

## Distinctions

Phonetic difference does not guarantee phonemic contrast. A phoneme is not an exact invariant waveform. Allophones need not always be in strict complementary distribution because variable realizations can occur.

## Scope and limits

The aspiration generalization is conditioned by syllable structure, stress, style and accent, not simply every written initial p. Other languages may use aspiration contrastively.

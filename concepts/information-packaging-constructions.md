# Information packaging constructions

## Key takeaway
English constructions can package similar propositional material differently by changing which material is presented as prominent, presupposed, topical or contrastive.

## Summary
Information packaging constructions include passives, clefts, existential presentations and marked fronting patterns when they are used to organize discourse information. Their interpretation depends on syntax, prosody and context; no single construction has one invariant discourse effect.

## Definition
An **information packaging construction** is a grammatical pattern whose form helps organize a message relative to alternatives, accessibility and discourse purposes. Packaging is distinct from the core participant situation: two clauses may describe closely related events while differing in grammatical functions, ordering or presuppositional profile.

## Distinctions
Information structure is not determined by word order alone. A passive does not automatically mark its Subject as Topic, and fronting does not automatically identify exhaustive Focus. Syntactic construction, prosodic realization and question under discussion must be recorded separately.

## Worked example
"A cyclist broke the window" and "The window was broken by a cyclist" can describe closely related events while presenting different grammatical starting points. "It was a CYCLIST who broke the window" introduces a cleft structure that can support a contrastive interpretation in an appropriate context.

## Scope and limits
Acceptability and discourse effects vary by construction, variety and context. The examples are schematic and do not supply a complete theory of clefts, passives, inversion or existential clauses.

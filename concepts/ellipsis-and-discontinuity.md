# Ellipsis and discontinuity

## Key takeaway
An English analysis may require understood but unpronounced material or dependencies whose related elements are not adjacent, so surface contiguity is not a universal guide to structure.

## Summary
Ellipsis concerns interpretations in which context licenses material that is not overtly pronounced. Discontinuity concerns dependencies whose related pieces are separated in the surface string. Both challenge analyses that equate syntactic structure with uninterrupted spans.

## Definition
**Ellipsis** is a family of constructions in which an interpretation depends on recoverable structure or content not fully pronounced. A **discontinuous dependency** relates positions or elements across intervening material, as in many wh-dependencies and displacement analyses.

## Distinctions
Not every omission is grammatical ellipsis: speakers can abandon utterances or rely on pragmatic enrichment. A gap notation is an analytical device, not an audible object. Frameworks differ on whether unpronounced syntax, direct interpretation or other mechanisms explain a case.

## Worked example
In "Maya can solve the first problem, and Leo can too", the second *can* is interpreted relative to preceding material. In "Which report did Maya say Leo filed?", the initial wh-expression is interpreted with a later position under common analyses.

## Scope and limits
English ellipsis and displacement include many construction-specific restrictions. The foundation records the problem space without committing every case to one derivation.

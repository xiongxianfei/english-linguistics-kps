# Auxiliary chain

## Key takeaway
English auxiliary constructions constrain the form of a following verb.

## Summary
English auxiliary constructions constrain the form of a following verb. The definitions and worked cases below state the intended analysis and its limits. This initial object is provisional; links deepen the explanation but do not replace it.

## Definition

English auxiliary constructions constrain the form of a following verb. In an ordinary finite chain the first auxiliary is finite, and the later forms reflect selection by preceding auxiliaries. Form, construction and semantic contribution are separate fields.

## Worked example

"The report may have been being reviewed" illustrates modal plus plain have; perfect HAVE plus participial been; progressive BE plus ing being; passive BE plus participial reviewed. The chain is stylistically heavy but useful for distinguishing all four relationships.

## Distinctions

An ing form is not itself progressive. A past participle is not itself passive. BE plus an apparent participle can also be adjectival, as in some readings of "The door is closed".

## Scope and limits

The chain order is a simplified description of core English auxiliaries, not an exhaustive treatment of dialectal modals or semi-auxiliaries. Tense, perfect, aspect and voice are not all one indivisible tense.

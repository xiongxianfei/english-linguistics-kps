# Lexical variation and register

## Key takeaway
English lexical choices vary across communities, situations, genres and historical periods, so a lexical entry must not be treated as uniform across all speakers and contexts.

## Summary
Lexical variation includes regional, social, stylistic, technical and diachronic differences in form, sense and distribution. Register concerns recurrent choices associated with activities and communicative settings, not a single scale from informal to formal.

## Definition
A lexical variant is an alternative form or sense whose distribution is associated with a variety, group, context or period. **Register** describes patterned linguistic choices associated with recurring situations, purposes and participant relationships.

## Distinctions
Variation is not automatically error, and frequency is not the same as grammatical possibility. Register differs from dialect, though they interact. A label such as *formal* or *regional* should be tied to evidence rather than inferred from personal familiarity.

## Worked example
English varieties may prefer different everyday terms for the same broad referent, while a technical community may use an ordinary-looking word with a specialized sense. A legal document and a casual conversation can select different lexical and multiword expressions even within the same regional variety.

## Scope and limits
The object supplies an analytical distinction, not a catalog of varieties or prescriptive recommendations. Detailed labels require dated corpus, community and genre evidence.

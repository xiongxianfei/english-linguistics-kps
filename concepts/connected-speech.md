# Connected speech

## Key takeaway
Connected-speech processes describe context-sensitive realization across segments and word boundaries.

## Summary
Connected-speech processes describe context-sensitive realization across segments and word boundaries. The definitions and worked cases below state the intended analysis and its limits. This initial object is provisional; links deepen the explanation but do not replace it.

## Definition

Connected-speech processes describe context-sensitive realization across segments and word boundaries. Assimilation changes similarity to a neighboring sound; elision omits an overt segment; reduction weakens material; linking connects articulation across a boundary.

## Worked example

In a possible realization of ten boys, the nasal can be bilabial under the influence of /b/. An unstressed function word may have a reduced vowel. In an apple, articulation can continue across the word boundary without a pause or change of lexical identity.

## Distinctions

Assimilation does not mean selecting a new lexeme or ignoring the underlying phonology. Linking need not delete any segment and alone is not evidence that a phone has disappeared.

## Scope and limits

Processes may be gradient and conditioned by speaking style, rate, accent and prosody. Orthographic examples do not establish which process occurred in a particular token; a recording is needed for token-specific claims.

# Grammaticality and acceptability

## Key takeaway
Grammaticality concerns licensing under a specified grammar or variety.

## Summary
Grammaticality concerns licensing under a specified grammar or variety. The definitions and worked cases below state the intended analysis and its limits. This initial object is provisional; links deepen the explanation but do not replace it.

## Definition

Grammaticality concerns licensing under a specified grammar or variety. Acceptability is a speaker's judgment in a particular task and context. Processing difficulty, unfamiliar vocabulary, plausibility and social expectations can affect acceptability without being grammatical constraints.

## Worked example

"Maya depends Leo" is excluded in the ordinary dependence construction of the standard English variety used here; "Maya depends on Leo" is licensed. A hearer may recover the intended message in the former. Conversely, a long nested relative construction can be structurally possible yet difficult to process.

## Distinctions

Understandable does not automatically mean grammatical. Unfamiliar, stigmatized or nonstandard does not mean linguistically defective. An asterisk records an analytical judgment for a stated variety, not a judgment of a speaker's worth.

## Scope and limits

No cross-dialect judgment or experimental acceptability result has been established by these invented examples. Records should separate analyst intuition, corpus attestation and elicited judgments.

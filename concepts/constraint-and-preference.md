# Constraint and preference

## Key takeaway
A grammatical licensing condition concerns what a stated grammar permits.

## Summary
A grammatical licensing condition concerns what a stated grammar permits. The definitions and worked cases below state the intended analysis and its limits. This initial object is provisional; links deepen the explanation but do not replace it.

## Definition

A grammatical licensing condition concerns what a stated grammar permits. An interpretive preference favors one of the available analyses given context and evidence. Plausibility, recoverability and grammaticality therefore must not be treated as synonyms.

## Worked example

DEPEND in the dependence construction licenses an on-PP, not a bare Object NP. A hearer can still infer the intention of "Maya depends Ravi". By contrast, phone versus notebook as the antecedent of "charged it" involves defeasible semantic and discourse evidence.

## Distinctions

A preference can be strong without being an absolute rule. A descriptive grammatical constraint is also scoped to a variety and construction, not a prescription about how every speaker must talk.

## Scope and limits

This draft provides no universal numeric ranking of constraints and no validated parser. Gradient acceptability and lexical-constructional variation are explicit review targets.

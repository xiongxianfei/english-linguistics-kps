# Quantification and scope

## Key takeaway
Quantification specifies how a claim ranges over a contextually relevant domain.

## Summary
Quantification specifies how a claim ranges over a contextually relevant domain. The definitions and worked cases below state the intended analysis and its limits. This initial object is provisional; links deepen the explanation but do not replace it.

## Definition

Quantification specifies how a claim ranges over a contextually relevant domain. Scope describes the relative interpretive reach of operators, not merely their printed order. This package uses classical first-order illustrations while marking their simplifications.

## Worked example

For "Every teacher recommended a book", EVERY > A means each teacher recommended at least one book; the books may coincide. A > EVERY means at least one book was recommended by every teacher; other books are not excluded. Different-books-required and exactly-one-book are stronger claims than either reading supplies.

## Distinctions

Not every teacher agreed means at least one relevant teacher did not agree; it does not guarantee that anyone agreed. No teacher agreed allows zero agree-ers. With an empty restrictor, universal statements are vacuously true in the classical model.

## Scope and limits

Scope availability depends on construction, context and theory. The existential baseline for some must be separated from plural-noun interpretation and scalar implicature. This note does not settle debates about inverse scope.

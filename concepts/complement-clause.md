# Complement clause

## Key takeaway
A complement clause occupies a position licensed by a head or construction.

## Summary
A complement clause occupies a position licensed by a head or construction. The definitions and worked cases below state the intended analysis and its limits. This initial object is provisional; links deepen the explanation but do not replace it.

## Definition

A complement clause occupies a position licensed by a head or construction. Its external function is distinct from its own finiteness, interrogative/declarative character and internal argument structure.

## Worked example

"Nina thinks that Ravi left" has a finite declarative complement. "Nina wonders whether Ravi left" has an interrogative complement. "Nina wants to leave" and "Nina enjoys reading" use nonfinite complements in this analysis.

## Distinctions

What introduces different constructions. "Nina knows what Ravi bought" may involve an embedded question; a superficially similar expression can be a fused relative elsewhere. Do not classify a clause only from its first word.

## Scope and limits

Control, raising and exceptional-case constructions require more than the simple complement label. The draft explicitly leaves competing subject analyses for advanced review.

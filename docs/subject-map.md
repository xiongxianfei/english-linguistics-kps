# English linguistics subject map

## Key takeaway
Find English knowledge by subject and follow the relationships between sounds, words, sentences, meaning and context.

## Summary
This authored map organizes the package's existing explanations around linguistic subjects. It includes thirteen sound-system topics and outlines word structure, lexical knowledge, syntax, semantics, pragmatics, discourse and information structure. Topics can share Concepts, Principles, Models and Methods. Practices select from this structure to build learning or investigation routes.

## Scope and source basis

The map covers the package's introductory English foundation. The groupings reuse established disciplinary distinctions while selecting material for this package; no single source prescribes this exact hierarchy. A topic can connect several areas, and its position does not imply a processing stage or a compulsory learning order. [Scope and theory policy](SCOPE.md) states the working frameworks and exclusions. New pronunciation examples use the declared General American convention; source-specific accent differences remain visible.

| Established account | Contribution to this map | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| [Anderson first edition](../references/anderson-2018-essentials-of-linguistics.md) | Selected accounts of articulation, transcription, contrast, sound processes, word forms, constituents and meaning | Only the recorded passages were inspected; Canadian examples do not define a full General American inventory |
| [Official IPA chart](../references/international-phonetic-association-undated-ipa-chart.md) | Symbol categories and notation, including consonant and vowel dimensions | A chart does not specify an English phoneme inventory or a speaker's realization |
| [MIT ToBI tutorial](../references/mit-2006-tobi-tutorial.md) | Selected distinctions between prominence, phrasing and tonal annotation | Selected PDF text only; no audio or complete ToBI course inspection |
| [Roberts on information structure](../references/roberts-2012-information-structure.md) | A questions-under-discussion account connecting context, focus and prosody | Abstract-level support only; detailed focus analyses remain provisional |
| [Mann and Taboada RST introduction](../references/mann-taboada-undated-rst-introduction.md) | Named relations between discourse spans | Selected website definitions; the package uses an authored subset |

Source records describe support for particular claims, not certification of every linked topic. Existing gaps are retained below. The [role index](INDEX.md) lists individual documents; this map groups them by subject. The [learning Practice](../practices/learn-english-linguistics.md) supplies activities and a learning route.

## Subject outline

- [Sound systems](#sound-systems): physical speech, sound categories, organization and contextual realization.
- [Word structure and lexical knowledge](#word-structure-and-lexical-knowledge): word identities, forms, internal structure and lexical properties.
- [Sentence structure](#sentence-structure): constituents, dependencies, functions and clause organization.
- [Meaning](#meaning): interpretation, predication, scope and semantic consequences.
- [Context and discourse](#context-and-discourse): reference in use, inference, connected discourse and information structure.
- [Connections across subjects](#connections-across-subjects): correspondences among these descriptions of the same expression.

For distinctions used throughout the map, see [expression and representation](../concepts/expression-and-representation.md), [evidence and analysis](../concepts/evidence-and-analysis.md), and [grammaticality and acceptability](../concepts/grammaticality-and-acceptability.md). They distinguish an expression from its analysis, a claim from its evidence, and a grammar's licensing conditions from judgments in use.

## Sound systems

The main accounts are [Phonetics](../models/english-phonetics-system.md), [Phonology](../models/english-phonology-system.md) and [Prosody](../models/english-prosody-system.md). The following thirteen topics provide a subject outline across them; each topic need not have its own file.

|---|---|---|

| 2 Phones Phonemes Allophones | Phonetic segments, contrastive categories and their variants; connects sound description with a phonological analysis | [Phone phoneme and allophone](../concepts/phone-phoneme-and-allophone.md), [realization and environment](../principles/realization-varies-with-environment.md) |

| 9 Phonotactics | Restrictions and preferences on sound positions and combinations; distinguishes structural possibility from the existence of a word | [Phonotactics](../concepts/phonotactics.md), [constraint and preference](../concepts/constraint-and-preference.md) |

Articulatory properties help describe differences; contrast and distribution ask how those differences function in a language. Syllables and phonotactics describe organization and constraints. Stress and prosody provide further context for realization. Connected-speech processes cross these divisions. The morphology interface connects the sound account to [word structure](#word-structure-and-lexical-knowledge), while [Information Structure Prosody](../models/focus-prosody-interface.md) connects prominence with [context](#context-and-discourse).

For investigation, [Analyze sound realization](../methods/analyze-sound-realization.md) keeps grammatical form, sound category and token evidence separate. Listening and production activities are in [Learn a word pronunciation](../practices/learn-word-pronunciation.md) and [Classify and produce a phone](../practices/classify-and-produce-phone.md).

**Source coverage:** Anderson 2.6–2.7 supports selected articulation distinctions; 3.1 transcription; 3.4 contextual aspiration; 3.5–3.8 selected processes and suprasegmentals; 4.1 contrast; 4.4 natural classes; and 6.2 allomorphy. IPA and MIT provide the bounded contributions above. Dedicated source attachment for the contrast/distribution Concept, syllable structure, phonotactics, the Connected Speech Concept and the morphology interface remains incomplete. Related Model citations do not automatically fill those gaps. Detailed focus evidence, complete accent inventories and observed speech remain further work.

## Boundaries and use

This map is the shared subject navigation for learning, investigation and reuse. Practices own their selected activities and order; the map owns the topic outline. Maintain this authored outline when subject coverage changes, and use the generated role index for the complete file inventory. Adding a link does not promote a provisional explanation to established evidence.

Language acquisition, sociolinguistic variation, historical change, psycholinguistic processing and corpus methods require further coverage beyond this foundation. Acoustic and perceptual investigation is only introduced, and the thirteen sound topics are not an exhaustive research taxonomy. The [learning Practice](../practices/learn-english-linguistics.md) currently develops one paper sound exercise; further learning units and actual learner outcomes remain separate work.

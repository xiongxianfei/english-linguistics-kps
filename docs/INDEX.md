# Knowledge index

## Key takeaway
Locate knowledge by its primary role and inspect the local explanation before applying it.

## Summary
This is an optional catalog of canonical Markdown, not another knowledge store. Each item has a local summary here; its full explanation, examples and limits remain in its owning file. All linguistic content is provisional.

## Concepts

**[Affix](../concepts/affix.md).** An affix is a bound morphological element attached to a base in the analysis adopted here.

**[Allomorphy](../concepts/allomorphy.md).** Allomorphy is variation in the forms realizing a morphological unit.

**[Ambiguity and vagueness](../concepts/ambiguity-and-vagueness.md).** Ambiguity involves distinguishable interpretations of a form or structure.

**[Anaphora and accessibility](../concepts/anaphora-and-accessibility.md).** Anaphora links an expression's interpretation to discourse material or a structurally available antecedent.

**[Auxiliary chain](../concepts/auxiliary-chain.md).** English auxiliary constructions constrain the form of a following verb.

**[Category and function](../concepts/category-and-function.md).** A syntactic category classifies an expression by its structural and distributional properties; a grammatical function describes its relationship within a larger construction.

**[Clause architecture](../concepts/clause-architecture.md).** The working clause representation distinguishes Subject from Predicate and then analyzes the predicate's head, complements and modifiers.

**[Cohesion and coherence](../concepts/cohesion-and-coherence.md).** Cohesion concerns linguistic linking devices such as reference chains, repetition, lexical relations, ellipsis and connectives.

**[Collocation](../concepts/collocation.md).** Collocation concerns conventional patterns of lexical co-occurrence.

**[Common ground](../concepts/common-ground.md).** Common ground is information participants mutually treat as accepted for the purposes of an interaction in a working discourse model.

**[Complement and modifier](../concepts/complement-and-modifier.md).** A complement is licensed by the relevant head or construction in a closer selectional relationship; a modifier contributes a less lexically restricted elaboration.

**[Complement clause](../concepts/complement-clause.md).** A complement clause occupies a position licensed by a head or construction.

**[Compositionality](../concepts/compositionality.md).** The compositionality principle relates the interpretation of a complex expression to interpretations of its parts and their mode of combination.

**[Compounding](../concepts/compounding.md).** Compounding combines lexical bases into a larger lexical expression.

**[Connected speech](../concepts/connected-speech.md).** Connected-speech processes describe context-sensitive realization across segments and word boundaries.

**[Consonant articulation](../concepts/consonant-articulation.md).** Consonants can be classified by voicing, place and manner of articulation.

**[Constituency](../concepts/constituency.md).** A constituent is a unit in a proposed hierarchical syntactic analysis.

**[Constraint and preference](../concepts/constraint-and-preference.md).** A grammatical licensing condition concerns what a stated grammar permits.

**[Contextual resolution](../concepts/contextual-resolution.md).** Contextual resolution identifies values needed to interpret an expression in its use, such as a pronoun referent, deictic place or relevant comparison class.

**[Contrast and concession](../concepts/contrast-and-concession.md).** Contrast presents a relevant difference or opposition between discourse units.

**[Contrast and distribution](../concepts/contrast-and-distribution.md).** A minimal pair differs in one relevant segment while contrasting lexical identity.

**[Coordination and subordination](../concepts/coordination-and-subordination.md).** Coordination combines expressions of comparable syntactic status; subordination makes a clause dependent within a larger structure.

**[Deixis](../concepts/deixis.md).** Deictic expressions anchor interpretation to features of an utterance context such as speaker, addressee, place or time.

**[Derivation](../concepts/derivation.md).** Derivation forms a related lexeme using a morphological pattern.

**[Discourse referent](../concepts/discourse-referent.md).** A discourse referent is a representation used to track an individual, group, event or other object through discourse.

**[Discourse relation](../concepts/discourse-relation.md).** A discourse relation links units through an interpretive relation such as explanation, result, elaboration, example, contrast or condition.

**[Do support and inversion](../concepts/do-support-and-inversion.md).** Ordinary English main-clause interrogatives and sentential negation interact with auxiliary syntax.

**[Evidence and analysis](../concepts/evidence-and-analysis.md).** An attested token, a constructed example, an elicited judgment, a source statement and an analyst's model are different evidence objects.

**[Expression and representation](../concepts/expression-and-representation.md).** A linguistic expression and an analysis of it are different objects.

**[Finiteness](../concepts/finiteness.md).** Finiteness classifies a clause or verbal element relative to tense, agreement and distributional properties in a particular grammar.

**[Focus topic and question under discussion](../concepts/focus-topic-and-qud.md).** Topic concerns what a discourse stretch is about; a question under discussion is the issue being addressed; focus relates an expression or domain to relevant alternatives.

**[Givenness and bridging](../concepts/givenness-and-bridging.md).** Givenness concerns contextual accessibility, not a permanent lexical property.

**[Grammaticality and acceptability](../concepts/grammaticality-and-acceptability.md).** Grammaticality concerns licensing under a specified grammar or variety.

**[Gricean reasoning](../concepts/gricean-reasoning.md).** Gricean analysis models conversational expectations involving informativeness, truthfulness or support, relevance and clarity.

**[Head and dependent](../concepts/head-and-dependent.md).** A head is the organizing element of a headed construction in a stated analysis.

**[Conversational implicature](../concepts/implicature.md).** Conversational implicature concerns communicated information beyond what is straightforwardly asserted under the relevant conventional interpretation.

**[Inflection](../concepts/inflection.md).** Inflection realizes grammatical distinctions within a lexeme's paradigm in the working lexeme-based model.

**[Linguistic interface](../concepts/interface.md).** An interface is a systematic correspondence or constraint between representations in different analytical systems.

**[Lexeme and word form](../concepts/lexeme-and-word-form.md).** A lexeme is an abstract lexical identity associated with a family of grammatical forms.

**[Lexical entry](../concepts/lexical-entry.md).** A lexical entry represents item-specific linguistic information relevant to a lexeme or sense: form, category, paradigm, meaning, selection, alternations and conventional combinations.

**[Lexical selection](../concepts/lexical-selection.md).** Selection relates lexical senses and constructions to allowable complements and their semantic properties.

**[Modality](../concepts/modality.md).** Modality evaluates possibilities or necessities relative to a source of constraints and, in many theories, an ordering of relevant alternatives.

**[Morpheme](../concepts/morpheme.md).** A morpheme is an abstract unit associated with a recurring grammatical or lexical contribution in a morphological analysis.

**[Negation and entailment](../concepts/negation-and-entailment.md).** Under a stated classical interpretation, P entails Q if every model making P true makes Q true.

**[Object and PP complement](../concepts/object-and-pp-complement.md).** A Direct Object and a PP complement are different syntactic realizations.

**[Paradigm and syncretism](../concepts/paradigm-and-syncretism.md).** A paradigm organizes the grammatical forms of a lexeme by feature values or form categories.

**[Phone phoneme and allophone](../concepts/phone-phoneme-and-allophone.md).** A phoneme is an abstract contrastive category in a language analysis.

**[Phonotactics](../concepts/phonotactics.md).** Phonotactics concerns restrictions and preferences on segment distributions and combinations in a language or variety.

**[Phrase and clause](../concepts/phrase-and-clause.md).** A phrase is a syntactic unit organized as a phrase category; it may contain one word or many.

**[Predicative complement](../concepts/predicative-complement.md).** A predicative complement attributes a property, classification or identity to a predicand rather than functioning as an ordinary Object participant.

**[Presupposition](../concepts/presupposition.md).** A presupposition is content treated as backgrounded in a use or associated with a triggering construction in a theory.

**[Productivity and lexicalization](../concepts/productivity-and-lexicalization.md).** Productivity concerns the extendability of a pattern to suitable new bases.

**[Prosody and intonation](../concepts/prosody.md).** Prosody organizes prominence, pitch patterns, timing and phrasing beyond individual segment identities.

**[Quantification and scope](../concepts/quantification-and-scope.md).** Quantification specifies how a claim ranges over a contextually relevant domain.

**[Reference and denotation](../concepts/reference-and-denotation.md).** Reference concerns what an expression is used to pick out in context; denotation is a semantic value under an interpretation.

**[Relative clause](../concepts/relative-clause.md).** An integrated relative clause modifies a nominal expression and establishes a dependency involving a position within that clause.

**[Orthography phonology and phonetics](../concepts/representation-levels.md).** Orthography describes writing; phonology describes language-specific sound organization; phonetics describes speech production, signals and perception.

**[Root base and stem](../concepts/root-base-and-stem.md).** A root is a minimally analyzable lexical core within the chosen morphological analysis.

**[Semantic predicate](../concepts/semantic-predicate.md).** A semantic predicate expresses a property or relation that combines with arguments.

**[Semantic role](../concepts/semantic-role.md).** A semantic role characterizes a participant's relationship to a predicate or situation.

**[Sense and lexical ambiguity](../concepts/sense-and-lexical-ambiguity.md).** A sense is a conventional interpretation associated with a lexical item in an analysis.

**[States events and lexical aspect](../concepts/situation-type.md).** Situation type concerns a predicate or construction's temporal and dynamic character.

**[Speech act](../concepts/speech-act.md).** A speech act is a communicative action performed through an utterance, such as asserting, asking, requesting or promising.

**[Stress](../concepts/stress.md).** Lexical stress is a prominence relation among syllables in a word.

**[Suppletion](../concepts/suppletion.md).** Suppletion describes paradigm-related forms whose relationship is not a regular productive phonological alternation in the synchronic system.

**[Syllable structure](../concepts/syllable-structure.md).** A working syllable model divides a syllable into onset and rhyme, with nucleus and coda inside the rhyme.

**[Temporal causal and evidential relations](../concepts/temporal-causal-and-evidential-relations.md).** Temporal relations locate situations before, after or overlapping others.

**[Tense and aspect](../concepts/tense-and-aspect.md).** Tense relates temporal reference to an evaluation context; aspect concerns temporal perspective and situation structure.

**[Utterance and speaker meaning](../concepts/utterance-and-speaker-meaning.md).** A sentence is a linguistic expression considered abstractly; an utterance is a particular use in context.

**[Valency and argument structure](../concepts/valency-and-argument-structure.md).** Valency describes a head's capacity for core arguments; argument structure represents the associated participant relations.

**[Vowel articulation](../concepts/vowel-articulation.md).** Vowel descriptions commonly use height, backness and lip rounding.

**[Wh dependency](../concepts/wh-dependency.md).** A wh-dependency links an interrogative or relative expression with a grammatical position in a clause.


## Principles

**[An accented word need not exhaust the focus domain](../principles/accent-does-not-exhaust-focus.md).** The same prominent word can participate in a narrow focus or a larger focused constituent.

**[Category does not determine grammatical function](../principles/category-does-not-determine-function.md).** A category label does not uniquely determine the grammatical function of its instances.

**[Cohesive links do not guarantee coherence](../principles/cohesion-does-not-guarantee-coherence.md).** Explicit linguistic links do not by themselves establish a well motivated conceptual relationship among all discourse units.

**[Context supplies values for context sensitive expressions](../principles/context-supplies-reference-values.md).** Conventional meaning can constrain reference without uniquely determining the referent of an utterance.

**[Regular inflectional exponents are conditioned by stem sounds](../principles/exponents-are-conditioned-by-sound.md).** Regular English plural and past endings vary with the relevant final sound of the base rather than its last written letter.

**[Grammatical functions do not determine semantic roles](../principles/functions-do-not-determine-roles.md).** A Subject need not be an Agent, and an Object need not be a Patient.

**[Conversational inferences can be defeasible](../principles/implicatures-are-defeasible.md).** A context-supported inference can be cancelled without cancelling the asserted existential content that supported it.

**[Local heads determine local dependency relations](../principles/local-heads-determine-local-relations.md).** A relation between an inner NP and its preposition must be distinguished from the relation between the whole PP and a higher head.

**[Phonetic realization varies with environment](../principles/realization-varies-with-environment.md).** A phonological category can have different phonetic realizations without a change of lexical identity.

**[Scope determines the interaction of semantic operators](../principles/scope-determines-operator-interaction.md).** Reversing quantifier or negation scope can change truth conditions while leaving overt words in the same order.

**[Complement selection is sensitive to lexical sense and construction](../principles/selection-is-sense-sensitive.md).** Knowing a word's lexical category alone does not determine all of its allowable complement patterns.

**[Temporal sequence does not establish causation](../principles/sequence-does-not-establish-cause.md).** An event following another in time does not by itself demonstrate that the earlier event caused it.

**[Structural validation does not establish linguistic truth](../principles/validation-does-not-establish-truth.md).** Passing publication checks establishes only the properties those checks actually inspect.


## Models

**[English discourse system](../models/english-discourse-system.md).** Discourse analysis connects utterances through referents, coherence relations and interaction while distinguishing text evidence from inferred interpretation.

**[English information structure system](../models/english-information-structure-system.md).** Information structure distinguishes topic, focus, givenness and the question under discussion rather than equating them with grammatical positions or accented words.

**[English lexicon system](../models/english-lexicon-system.md).** The lexicon connects a word's identity and forms with its senses, grammatical possibilities and conventional uses. This Model helps a reader explain why knowing a translation or a spelling is not enough to use or analyze a word. It connects lexical entries, ambiguity, categories, selection, meaning relations, combinations and register through worked examples and interfaces with Morphology, Syntax and Semantics. The entry format is an analytical tool, not a claim that dictionaries or brains store information in these exact fields. The coverage is introductory; its organization is an authored synthesis of selected sources and existing explanations. Examples are constructed for ordinary standard-English readings unless stated otherwise. The Model remains provisional and independently unreviewed.

**[English morphology system](../models/english-morphology-system.md).** Morphology explains word-internal structure, grammatical forms and lexical identity. Use this Model to distinguish a new lexeme from a grammatical form, justify a proposed segmentation, and explain why the same grammatical contribution can have different realizations. Its introductory coverage follows the outline's five areas: word identity and form, internal structure, inflection and word formation, alternation and lexical limits, and allomorphy versus allophony. The examples are constructed analyses; a complete English morphological inventory and independent review remain outside the present scope.

**[English phonetics system](../models/english-phonetics-system.md).** Phonetics describes speech production, the acoustic signal and perception; a written transcription is an analysis, not a recording.

**[English phonology system](../models/english-phonology-system.md).** Phonology models contrast, distribution and the organization of sound patterns within a specified language variety.

**[English pragmatics system](../models/english-pragmatics-system.md).** Separate what an expression contributes, what context resolves, what is treated as background, what is inferred and what action is attempted. Explain the assumptions connecting these parts.

**[English prosody system](../models/english-prosody-system.md).** Prosody organizes prominence, phrasing, timing and intonation across segments; no single pitch movement uniquely determines meaning.

**[English semantics system](../models/english-semantics-system.md).** Semantics explains how expressions contribute meaning, how those contributions combine, and what follows under a stated interpretation. A reader should be able to distinguish two readings, represent their truth conditions, and find a counterexample to an unsupported inference. Understanding what a sentence means is different from knowing whether it is true in the actual world. Thirteen visible topics connect meaning and reference, semantic building blocks, events and roles, composition, operators, time and the Syntax interface. Their order supports reading; the topics overlap and do not form a compulsory processing sequence. The recurring report-reading example shows how those distinctions work together. The finite representations are teaching models; their logical behavior does not establish which readings English speakers accept in every context. Examples are constructed, and the source support and remaining gaps are identified below.

**[English syntax system](../models/english-syntax-system.md).** Syntax explains how words form phrases and clauses, which expressions depend on which, and how constructions organize grammatical functions. Use this Model to build and justify a structural analysis: identify complete units, distinguish their categories from their functions, explain verb forms and clause relationships, and compare alternative groupings. A connected student-and-printer example develops from a simple clause into a sentence with auxiliaries and embedded clauses. The introductory coverage follows the outline's six areas: categories and functions, constituents and local dependencies, phrases and clauses, complement constructions, verb and auxiliary structure, and clause combination and dependencies. Interfaces connect these explanations with Morphology, Lexicon, Semantics and Prosody. The examples are constructed for ordinary standard-English readings. The account remains provisional; it uses NP terminology and a descriptive Subject/Predicate analysis rather than claiming one universal syntactic framework.

**[Analyze complete utterance](../methods/analyze-complete-utterance.md).** Produce a multi-level analysis whose representations remain distinct and whose interfaces are explicit.

**[Analyze constituency](../methods/analyze-constituency.md).** Record candidate constituent boundaries and local relations using converging diagnostics.

**[Analyze sound realization](../methods/analyze-sound-realization.md).** Distinguish morphological exponents, phonological categories and observed phonetic realizations.

**[Compare argument mappings](../methods/compare-argument-mappings.md).** Separate participant roles from grammatical functions when comparing constructions.

**[Trace discourse interpretation](../methods/trace-discourse-interpretation.md).** Track reference and discourse relations while distinguishing asserted information from defeasible inference.


## Practices

**[Classify and produce a phone](../practices/classify-and-produce-phone.md).** Identify what the input represents, build a justified articulatory description, then listen and attempt the sound without confusing a chart label, a phoneme or an observed token.

**[Edit English and learn from revisions](../practices/edit-english-and-learn.md).** Improve a piece of English for its intended reader, preserve what its author means, and explain the useful linguistic reason behind each substantive change.

**[Investigate linguistic question](../practices/investigate-linguistic-question.md).** Turn a bounded English question into a transparent provisional analysis and a reviewable evidence trail.

**[Learn English linguistics](../practices/learn-english-linguistics.md).** Learn to explain and investigate English examples by connecting established linguistic distinctions, their conditions and the evidence for an analysis.

**[Learn a word pronunciation](../practices/learn-word-pronunciation.md).** Learn one General American word by connecting its intended meaning, checked phonemic form, listening evidence, articulatory actions, recorded attempts and a later unprompted check.

**[Maintain English package](../practices/maintain-english-package.md).** Review changes and publish a coherent provisional package without confusing file integrity with linguistic truth.

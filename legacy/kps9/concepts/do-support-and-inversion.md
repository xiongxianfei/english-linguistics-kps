# Do support and inversion

## Key takeaway
Ordinary English main-clause interrogatives and sentential negation interact with auxiliary syntax.

## Summary

## Definition

Ordinary English main-clause interrogatives and sentential negation interact with auxiliary syntax. When an ordinary lexical verb lacks a suitable auxiliary, DO can support the construction and carries the finite inflection.

## Worked example

"Nina works" becomes "Does Nina work?" or "Nina does not work". With an auxiliary, "Nina has left" becomes "Has Nina left?". A neutral Subject wh-question "Who left?" does not require support DO. "Who DID leave?" can be emphatic rather than ungrammatical.

## Distinctions

Not every question inverts: embedded "I wonder where Nina went" ordinarily retains Subject-before-verb order. BE can invert in copular clauses without progressive meaning.

## Scope and limits

The restrictions are scoped to the constructions and varieties described. Negation types, emphatic DO, echo questions and historical usage need separate treatments; do not promote beginner transformation instructions into universal grammar.

# Lexical selection

## Key takeaway
Selection relates lexical senses and constructions to allowable complements and their semantic properties.

## Summary
Selection relates lexical senses and constructions to allowable complements and their semantic properties. The definitions and worked cases below state the intended analysis and its limits. This initial object is provisional; links deepen the explanation but do not replace it.

## Definition

Selection relates lexical senses and constructions to allowable complements and their semantic properties. Category selection, preposition selection and semantic compatibility answer different questions, though they interact in an utterance.

## Worked example

"The outcome depends on the weather" contains an on-PP complement, not a Direct Object of DEPEND. "Nina discussed the outcome" has an NP Object. In "Nina drank the chair", an ordinary literal interpretation clashes with the drinkable-object expectation even though Subject-Verb-Object order is available.

## Distinctions

The recoverability of a message does not license every structure. Conversely, a metaphorical interpretation can change semantic compatibility without proving that syntactic constraints never matter.

## Scope and limits

Judgments are for the specified ordinary standard-English senses. Constructions, metaphor and varieties can differ; do not label an entire language community ungrammatical from a narrow teaching rule.

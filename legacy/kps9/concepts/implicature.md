## Definition

Conversational implicature concerns communicated information beyond what is straightforwardly asserted under the relevant conventional interpretation. Context, alternatives and communicative expectations support the inference. Cancellability is a useful diagnostic, not a complete theory.

## Worked example

"Some researchers agreed" can suggest not all. "Some agreed; in fact, all did" can cancel that suggestion without denying existential agreement. With limited speaker knowledge, the inference may instead be that the speaker cannot say whether all agreed.

## Distinctions

Failure to assert all does not logically entail belief in not all without assumptions about information and competence. Plural some researchers and singular some researcher also raise separate plurality questions.

## Scope and limits

Implicatures are context-dependent and can be conventionalized in ordinary usage without becoming semantic entailments. Not every inferred proposition is something the speaker intended to implicate.

# States events and lexical aspect

## Key takeaway
Situation type concerns a predicate or construction's temporal and dynamic character.

## Summary
Situation type concerns a predicate or construction's temporal and dynamic character. The definitions and worked cases below state the intended analysis and its limits. This initial object is provisional; links deepen the explanation but do not replace it.

## Definition

Situation type concerns a predicate or construction's temporal and dynamic character. States hold; activities unfold; accomplishments include development toward an endpoint; achievements highlight a transition. This expanded scheme refines the introductory state/event contrast.

## Worked example

"Maya owns a flat" describes a state; "Maya walked" an activity; "Maya wrote a report" normally supplies an endpoint; "The glass broke" describes a change without making the glass an Agent. Adding "a report" can affect aspectual interpretation compared with simply writing.

## Distinctions

Event does not mean intentional action, completed event or past event. Syntactic Subject-Verb-Object order alone cannot decide situation type, and lexical spelling alone does not settle sense-specific aspect.

## Scope and limits

Aspectual classifications interact with arguments, adverbials and context. These labels are introductory diagnostics, not experimentally established categories for every use of a verb.

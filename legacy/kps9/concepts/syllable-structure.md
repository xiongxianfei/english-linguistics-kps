# Syllable structure

## Key takeaway

## Definition

A working syllable model divides a syllable into onset and rhyme, with nucleus and coda inside the rhyme. The nucleus is commonly a vowel but may be a syllabic consonant. Open means no coda; closed means a coda is present.

## Worked example

For spin /spɪn/: onset /sp/, nucleus /ɪ/, coda /n/, closed. For see /siː/: onset /s/, nucleus /iː/, no coda, open. For about /əˈbaʊt/, the second nucleus is the diphthong /aʊ/, not two syllables.

## Distinctions

Spelling does not count nuclei. Team has two written vowel letters but one ordinary syllable; make contains silent e. Strengths alone is not a counterexample to one-written-vowel/one-syllable because it has only one written vowel.

## Scope and limits

Syllabification between syllables can be disputed, and syllabic consonants defeat an absolute vowel-only nucleus definition. The onset-rhyme model is one useful representation, not the sole theory.

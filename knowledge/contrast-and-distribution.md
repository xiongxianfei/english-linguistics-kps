## Definition

A minimal pair differs in one relevant segment while contrasting lexical identity. Contrastive distribution permits alternatives in comparable environments with lexical contrast. Complementary distribution describes nonoverlapping environments, which can support allophony with additional evidence.

## Worked example

Pin /pɪn/ and bin /bɪn/ isolate a contrast. Cat and dog do not. Predictable [pʰ] in one environment and [p] in another support a context-conditioned realization analysis. A missing minimal pair alone proves neither identity nor difference of phonemes.

## Distinctions

Distribution is the evidence; phoneme grouping is the analysis. Complementary distribution of phonetically unrelated sounds is not automatically an allophonic relationship.

## Scope and limits

The categories are simplified. Near-minimal pairs, alternations, phonotactics and speaker judgments can matter; free variation is often socially or stylistically conditioned rather than literally free of all conditions.

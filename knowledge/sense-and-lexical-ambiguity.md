## Definition

A sense is a conventional interpretation associated with a lexical item in an analysis. Polysemy concerns related senses; homonymy concerns distinct lexical identities sharing a form. The boundary cannot be determined mechanically from spelling.

## Worked example

RUN can describe locomotion, managing an organization, or machinery functioning. A constructed sentence "Maya approached the bank" leaves a financial-institution versus river-edge reading open without further context. The lexical alternatives differ even if the broad syntactic template stays fixed.

## Distinctions

A contextual inference is not automatically a new dictionary sense. Conversely, shared pronunciation does not establish a common lexeme. Source-based lexicographic and distributional evidence is required for difficult cases.

## Scope and limits

The examples illustrate possible analyses; no historical claim or exhaustive sense inventory is made. Different dictionaries and theories can group conventional uses differently.

## Definition

Cohesion concerns linguistic linking devices such as reference chains, repetition, lexical relations, ellipsis and connectives. Coherence concerns an interpretable organization of a text or interaction. The terms should not be identified respectively with semantics and pragmatics.

## Worked example

"Maya bought a telescope. She likes coffee. The telescope was costly" has clear anaphoric links but a potentially poorly motivated middle contribution. "The alarm sounded. Everyone left" can invite a coherent causal sequence without an explicit causal connective.

## Distinctions

Coherence can be established by a larger task even when local topics change. Lack of an immediately apparent relation is not proof that no possible discourse context would make the passage coherent.

## Scope and limits

RST supplies one explicit relation-based framework. Cohesion taxonomies and coherence theories differ, and this draft does not claim that a count of pronouns measures textual quality.

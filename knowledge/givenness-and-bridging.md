# Givenness and bridging

## Definition

Givenness concerns contextual accessibility, not a permanent lexical property. An expression may be discourse-new but inferable from an established scenario. Bridging typically links a newly evoked referent to another referent through a relation rather than strict identity.

## Worked example

"Maya entered a house. The kitchen was small" permits a house-to-kitchen inference. The kitchen was not explicitly introduced and is not the house itself. It is useful to label it inferable or accessible-by-bridging rather than force every theory to call it Given.

## Distinctions

Given does not mean pronoun; a full NP can retrieve an accessible referent. Given does not mean background: "THE SERVER, not the printer" can focus an already mentioned alternative. Microscope to the instrument can be coreference, not a distinct bridging entity.

## Scope and limits

Different givenness theories distinguish discourse-old, hearer-old, inferable and active statuses. Salience can decay without deleting a discourse referent from memory or common ground.

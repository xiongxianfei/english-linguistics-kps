## Definition

Deictic expressions anchor interpretation to features of an utterance context such as speaker, addressee, place or time. Their conventional interpretation instructions remain distinguishable from the contextual values assigned in a use.

## Worked example

If Maya says "I will meet you here tomorrow", I normally identifies Maya, you identifies her addressee, here the relevant location, and tomorrow the day after the utterance day. Quotation or narrative perspective can introduce a different context for interpretation.

## Distinctions

The pronoun I does not lexically mean Agent: in "I received a prize" the referent is Recipient. Here does not permanently mean a city or even the exact physical location of the speaker.

## Scope and limits

Pointing, demonstratives and shifted narration complicate a simple speaker-now-here model. Reference depends on contextual anchoring, not arbitrary selection of any plausible entity.

## Definition

Prosody organizes prominence, pitch patterns, timing and phrasing beyond individual segment identities. Pitch is perceived highness or lowness, linked to fundamental frequency in voiced speech, not the same as loudness. Intonation organizes pitch over phrases.

## Worked example

A wh-question can end in a fall, while a declarative used to seek confirmation can rise. For "Who repaired it?", ANNA may receive prominence. For "What did Anna do?", a larger VP focus can be realized with strongest accent on its final content word.

## Distinctions

Punctuation does not determine pauses. Topic is not necessarily focus, and the accented word does not necessarily exhaust the focus domain. A phonological accent pattern must be distinguished from its acoustic realization.

## Scope and limits

No audio has been measured for this draft. Intonational meanings, focus projection and rhythm are dialect- and context-sensitive. English is not assumed to have perfectly equal intervals between stresses.

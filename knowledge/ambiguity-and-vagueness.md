## Definition

Ambiguity involves distinguishable interpretations of a form or structure. Vagueness involves borderline application of an interpretation. Contextual underspecification concerns missing contextual values and need not amount to two discrete lexical meanings.

## Worked example

"Maya saw the student with binoculars" can attach the PP to the NP or the seeing event. "Every student read a paper" permits scope analyses. Tall has context-sensitive standards and borderline cases. Without context, "She arrived" may simply leave the referent unidentified.

## Distinctions

Lexical, structural, scope and referential alternatives locate different sources of ambiguity. Evidence that favors an interpretation is not the same as the subsystem that made it available.

## Scope and limits

The taxonomies overlap and depend on theory. Prosody can help without guaranteeing a unique attachment, and a source of uncertainty should not automatically be attributed to syntax.

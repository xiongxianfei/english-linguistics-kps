# General American pronunciation policy

## Key takeaway
General American is the primary accent reference for new pronunciation analyses, but phonemic notation, phonetic realizations, evidence, and accent variation remain distinct.

## Summary
This package uses conventional broad General American English transcription as its default for new English pronunciation examples. Sounds American supplies selected instructional articulation demonstrations; toPhonetics supplies candidate broad transcriptions with its American option selected; the International Phonetic Association chart identifies general IPA symbols. None is a complete authority for every accent, lexical item, or recorded token. All linguistic analyses remain provisional until claim-level review.

## Primary variety and notation
- Label the target as **General American** when writing new English pronunciation examples. This is a descriptive reference variety, not one invariant voice or a judgment about other dialects.
- Use angle brackets for orthography, slashes for broad phonemic notation, and square brackets for phonetic descriptions. Choose transcription detail according to the research question; do not call predicted narrow IPA an observed recording.
- In the default American broad convention, FLEECE is commonly written /i/ and GOOSE /u/ rather than the British-style /iː/ and /uː/. Do not infer a phonetic absence of vowel-length differences from omitted phonemic length marks.
- The reference variety is normally rhotic. A broad /r/ may stand for the American rhotic consonant, whose common approximant realization is described more closely as [ɹ]; do not treat the IPA trill [r] as the obligatory American phone.
- Conventional transcriptions may use rhoticized-vowel symbols such as /ɚ/ and /ɝ/ or vowel plus /r/ sequences. State the convention used; do not silently equate all analyses.
- Flag mergers (including cot and caught), vowel fronting, flapping, reduction, and other variation where material. No word has one obligatory realization across every American speaker and context.

## Illustrative transcriptions
| Word | General American broad example | Representation caution |
|---|---|---|
| see | /si/ | FLEECE vowel; compare British-style /siː/ without treating the spelling difference as an exact duration measurement |
| food | /fud/ | GOOSE vowel; actual tongue position and duration vary |
| car | /kɑr/ | Rhotic example; non-rhotic British /kɑː/ belongs to a different accent model |
| pin | /pɪn/ | A possible stressed initial realization is [pʰɪn], not entailed by the broad transcript alone |
| about | /əˈbaʊt/ | Primary stress precedes the second syllable; the first contains a reduced vowel |

These are **authored illustrative examples**, not transcriptions copied from a provider or recordings of particular speakers. The use of /i/ and /u/ is a declared notation convention, not an attempt to replace the general IPA chart.

## Three source roles
1. **Articulatory learning:** [Sounds American](https://soundsamerican.net/) describes and demonstrates selected General American sounds. Consult the [source record](references/sounds-american-undated-chart-and-terms.md) for the limited inspection and rights statement. Do not redistribute videos, audio, images, or adapted charts.
2. **Candidate lexical transcription:** [toPhonetics](https://tophonetics.com/) with **American** selected provides broad transcription suggestions, possible reading alternatives, and optional weak forms. Its own [FAQ](https://tophonetics.com/faq/) says the American data was initially based on the Carnegie Mellon Pronouncing Dictionary and acknowledges database corrections. Consult the [source record](references/tophonetics-undated-english-transcription.md). Do not promote a tool result to an independently verified claim.
3. **General phone and IPA symbol definitions:** the [Interactive IPA Chart](https://www.internationalphoneticassociation.org/IPAcharts/IPA_charts_EI/IPA_charts_EI.html) helps identify phone symbols in the cross-language phonetic system. Its [source record](references/international-phonetic-association-undated-interactive-chart.md) distinguishes interactive-content and audio rights from the separately licensed static IPA chart. It does not choose the General American phoneme inventory.

## Reproducible lookup and analysis
1. Identify the intended lexeme, lexical sense, grammatical form and context, including stress where relevant.
2. Declare the accent **General American** and whether the target is broad phonemic /.../ or narrow phonetic [...] transcription.
3. Obtain a **candidate** American broad transcription from toPhonetics and check alternative readings, homographs, and its weak-form setting. Note when the tool cannot resolve an item.
4. Cross-check disputed lexical forms with a suitable independent pronunciation dictionary or reliable accent-specific linguistic description before treating an entry as verified. Do not assume two sources derived from the same pronunciation dictionary are independent evidence.
5. For an individual sound, use Sounds American to understand instructional articulation and the IPA chart to verify symbol identity, voicing, place, manner, or vowel quality. Neither replaces a contextually justified lexical analysis.
6. For surface realizations such as [ɾ] or [pʰ], distinguish a predicted contextual phone from a **recorded and inspected** token. A source video or browser text-to-speech playback is not evidence of how a specific speaker pronounced a different utterance.
7. Record the source, URL, date, inspected extent, phonemic convention, proposed form, uncertainty, and counterexamples in the associated KPS object. Retain provisional status pending actual claim-level review.

## Historical and alternate varieties
Existing British-style symbols in older draft objects are not silently interpreted as General American. Either relabel them as a British illustrative convention, or create a separately supported GA counterpart before using the example as a default. Do not perform blind symbol substitution on entire word transcriptions: lexical incidence, mergers, rhoticity, vowels and stress can differ across accents. An update to the primary reference variety does not invalidate other English varieties.

## Evidence and rights limits
This is a **repository authoring policy**, not an independently verified pronunciation inventory. Website summaries, chart symbol identities, and tool capabilities were inspected to the extents stated in the Reference records; this does not validate every transcribed word or speaker. The sources have different rights: the Sounds American site generally specifies CC BY-NC-ND for its material; the IPA interactive chart has its own restrictions and contributor audio rights; the separately downloadable static IPA chart may be CC BY-SA. Link rather than mirror third-party material. Refer to [NOTICE](NOTICE.md) for repository licensing.
